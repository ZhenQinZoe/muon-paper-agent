1. Byzantine-Robust Muon: Resilient Momentum Orthogonalization for Distributed Learning
Authors: Grigory Malinovsky, Yury Demidovich, Peter Richtárik
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13137v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 18.5
Abstract: Optimization methods, including the Muon optimizer based on the Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO) and momentum orthogonalization, have shown strong empirical performance in large-scale and distributed learning, yet their robustness to adversarial behavior and data heterogeneity is not well understood. We study these methods within a unified first-order trust-region framework and analyze Muon-based updates with robust aggregation for Byzantine-robust distributed and federated learning. Our analysis does not rely on the i.i.d. assumption and introduces a non-Euclidean notion of heterogeneity consistent with the trust-region geometry. Under this framework, we establish convergence guarantees for both general non-convex and star-convex objectives. Our results provide a theoretical characterization of Byzantine-robust Muon-based optimization, with an additive heterogeneity error floor consistent with known impossibility results, under explicit aggregation assumptions. Under an explicit additive oracle-error condition, the guarantees also cover inexact orthogonalization, with the non-convex Byzantine terms in the bound's numerator unchanged but a smaller progress denominator. In simulated federated training with ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny on CIFAR-10, additional image benchmarks, and a 38M-parameter byte-level language model on TinyStories, the tested Muon configuration attains higher attacked accuracy (lower perplexity) than the tested SGD and normalized-SGD configurations, and than sign updates on CIFAR-10. Fixed-honest controls further show a substantial RFA advantage over averaging under a bounded RFA-query attack, higher attacked accuracy for Muon-NS than tensorwise Euclidean normalization from shared checkpoints, and lower held-out TinyStories perplexity than a tuned AdamW baseline.

2. Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits
Authors: Hadi Salloum, Ammar Ali
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Weight-space programming, introduced in recent MIT work by Takehi et al., enables a frontier LLM agent to read a network's weights and write a repair. Their GPT-6 Astra agent corrected the reasoning loops of Qwen3.5-0.8B without gradients, using 158 agent turns, 89.7 million tokens, and 50 forward-pass requests. Our method, Tree-Ising, casts this repair as a sparse discrete optimization problem over the model's own BF16 weights. Forward statistics define an objective that weakens a loop direction, strengthens a concluding direction, and preserves the layer's output on productive tokens. Within a state-tree representation, where lossless BF16 storage acts as a special case, the admissible edits are sign flips and exponent shifts that preserve all shared group exponents and mantissas. We decompose this objective exactly into independent QUBO (Ising) problems, one per output row, which we solve on a GPU using simulated annealing or quantum-inspired simulated bifurcation. On the agent's two layers, Tree-Ising achieves 54.6% on MATH-500, outperforming the agent's edit (51.7%) under the same evaluation protocol, while the base model scores 7.9% as reported. The simulated bifurcation solver yields a statistically indistinguishable repair at 55.1%. Both solvers outperform the agent edit across all five math benchmarks, with statistically significant improvements on MATH-500 and GSM8K after correcting for multiple comparisons. Our approach modifies 13% to 17% fewer weights and reduces BF16 bit flips by a factor of 2.2 to 2.3. It requires only four forward-pass requests and zero LLM tokens, with the annealing recipe costing about $0.90 in GPU time. On Qwen3.5-2B, with layers and dose selected using only the base model, the same recipe increases greedy GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%. As a proof of concept, we execute an eight-variable repair subproblem using QAOA on an IBM Heron processor.

3. Cross-Border Fulfillment Center Selection via Doubly Robust Estimation and Linear Programming
Authors: Kriti Mahajan, Patrick Bloebaum
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14174v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: We introduce the cross-border (XB) fulfillment center (FC) Selection Model: a multi-stage optimization framework combining forecasting, doubly robust estimation, and linear programming to select optimal source FCs. For each source-destination shipping arc, the objective is to choose a subset of source-country FCs that minimize delivery time and cost while maximizing profit and shipped units under a fixed domestic network. We evaluate the model on three production-scale international shipping corridors for a large e-commerce company using 12 months of shipment data, employing both aggregate offline replay and shipment-level counterfactual nearest-neighbor matching. Across all shipping corridors, the XB FC Selection Model achieves 8.5-51.1% reductions in delivery time and 4.4-48.3% reductions in logistics cost relative to human expert-defined FC selection policies, with up to 6.5 percentage-point improvements in 5/5 target compliance (5 days, $5 USD). Pareto analysis shows the model consistently dominates single-objective ranking strategies and unconstrained LP on the efficiency-reallocation frontier, with all results statistically significant.

4. HaiRuo: Forecasting Ocean Eddies as Fields and Objects with a Satellite-Altimetry Foundation Model
Authors: Xukun Qin, Haochen Sun, Yuyang Tian, Ge Chen
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14136v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Mesoscale eddies carry most of the ocean's kinetic energy and move heat, salt and nutrients across basins, yet where an eddy will be two weeks ahead is still read off ocean models with post-hoc tracking heuristics. We introduce HaiRuo, which forecasts eddies as fields and as objects at once: from 30 days of satellite sea-level maps, one forward pass returns 15 days of sea level and geostrophic velocity, daily eddy masks and the trajectory of every eddy alive today. HaiRuo pairs two components with a clear division of labour: (1) HaiRuo-1B, an open-weight 1.05-billion-parameter foundation model pretrained without labels on 24 years of global satellite-altimetry observations, which supplies the 15-day physical fields and masks (+0.14 field skill and +1.1 mIoU points under the same decoder) and further lowers 15-day centre error by 1.2 km frozen (1.35 km over training from scratch); and (2) XuanJi, a Lagrangian object decoder that samples the evolving forecast in each eddy's co-moving polar frame and refines the 15-day path across three look-ahead rounds, removing 2.5 km on HaiRuo-1B (2.9 km with joint fine-tuning) and 2.5-3.3 km via its object attention across six published backbones. Under one pre-registered global evaluation over the whole ocean, HaiRuo improves on fourteen methods on every endpoint (25.2 vs. 30.6 km 15-day centre error, -18%), holding its margin outside the labelled regions and through both phases of the El Nino-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), while reproducing westward drift, 50-60 deg S eastward reversal and poleward cyclone-anticyclone deflection. Over ten days through one fixed tracker, HaiRuo's forecast field cuts the eddy-centre error of WenHai and XiHe by 37-43% (-2.8 km vs. day 0 while they degrade +4.3-8.7 km), and its object trajectories cut pipeline error by 77-78% globally. Open-weight checkpoints, code and project page: https://github.com/xkqin/HaiRuo

5. Resilient Integrated Access and Backhaul Network Planning via Graph Neural Network Curriculum Learning
Authors: Jie Zhang, Haochen Sun, Mingzhang Zhou, Paul D. Mitchell, Hamed Ahmadi, Qiang Ni
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14042v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Integrated Access and Backhaul (IAB) is a key enabler for cost-effective millimeter-wave (mmWave) densification, yet practical deployments are strongly constrained by the spatial availability of fiber-connected IAB donors. Variations in donor topologies reshape both the initial coverage footprint and the feasible multi-hop backhaul expansion patterns, causing planning policies trained on a single topology to generalize poorly. This paper studies cross-topology generalization for resilience-aware IAB deployment under coverage and transport feasibility requirements. We formulate IAB deployment as a constrained Markov decision process that couples access coverage satisfaction with multi-hop transport feasibility, including hop-by-hop rate-budget degradation and a minimum parent-connectivity requirement for fault tolerance. To solve the resulting large discrete planning problem, we propose a graph-based actor-critic approach that uses a Graph Attention Network v2 (GATv2) encoder with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and we develop a multi-stage curriculum learning strategy that progressively increases constraint difficulty and scenario diversity for stable training and transferable policies. In a 60 GHz urban simulation over a 1000 m x 1000 m area with multiple donor layouts, the trained policy satisfies the minimum coverage requirement of 98% with fewer deployed IAB nodes than learning and heuristic baselines, and maintains higher coverage under progressive backhaul failures. The results demonstrate that curriculum-trained graph-based reinforcement learning can improve deployment efficiency and resilience while transferring across heterogeneous IAB donor topologies without retraining.
