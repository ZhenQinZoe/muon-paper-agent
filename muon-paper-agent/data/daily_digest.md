## Top Highlights

1. DMuon: Scaling Matrix Optimizers in Data Parallelism with Topology-Aware Matrix Ownership and Communication
Authors: Starrick Liu, Ruizhi Chengze, Lufang Chen, Michael Zhao, Ran Yu, Xiaofan Li
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07084v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 17.0
Abstract: Matrix optimizers such as Muon update whole weight matrices, which conflicts with the element- or row-wise sharding of data-parallel runtimes. Once parameters are sharded as well, matrix optimizers incur extra communication or repeated computation. We present DMuon, a matrix-granular optimizer runtime for PyTorch FSDP (ZeRO-2/3) and HSDP. DMuon assigns each matrix a unique owner, which performs the Newton--Schulz (NS) iteration and optimizer update on the full matrix, and balances the load across owners by computational cost. On HSDP, ownership is global across replicas, so the cluster keeps a single copy of gradients and optimizer state, and updated parameters are published across nodes as soon as they are ready, overlapping subsequent computation. This layout makes every collective sparse and variable in length, so DMuon schedules these collectives as their data becomes ready and routes them along the cluster's two-level network. On up to 256 H100 GPUs, across three dense LLMs and two VLA models, DMuon matches or exceeds the training throughput of PyTorch FSDP2 with AdamW, reaching 1.05$\times$ that of FSDP2 with AdamW at the median on 256 GPUs. The optimized parameter gathering and gradient reduction absorb the extra communication and computation a matrix optimizer requires. Against a gather-then-replicate Muon baseline, DMuon's throughput is 2.1 times as high at the median.

2. Module-Aware Optimization for Graph Neural Networks
Authors: Guy Hadad, Haggai Roitman, Moshe Eliasof, Bracha Shapira
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08379v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 14.0
Abstract: Different optimizers impose different geometries on parameter updates. Adaptive methods such as Adam and AdamW use coordinate-wise updates, while matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon utilize weight-matrix structure through orthogonalized updates. For Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), however, the effective geometry of its weight matrices is shaped not only by its learned channel-mixing weights, but also by the graph-propagated representations it acts on. Thus, even if an update is well normalized as a matrix, its effect on the layer output can be amplified when applied to graph-propagated features. To address this gap, we introduce GraphMuon, a graph-aware optimizer that adapts Muon's matrix updates to the covariance structure of graph-propagated layer inputs. GraphMuon performs orthogonalization in a metric defined by these propagated representations and normalizes step sizes according to the update's induced effect on the layer representation. We show that theoretically, vanilla Muon can suffer from graph-induced update amplification, while our GraphMuon can mitigate this effect. As such, our GraphMuon yields a graph-metric analogue of Muon's polar update step. Empirically, GraphMuon achieves $1.32\times$-$2.42\times$ validation-loss time-to-target speedups over AdamW on three large-scale OGB benchmarks. Across 15 dataset-architecture configurations spanning node classification, inductive graph classification, and graph regression, it achieves lower time-to-target in 13 cases, while maintaining competitive or superior predictive performance. Code is available at https://github.com/guy-hadad/GraphMuon.

3. The Quantization Benefits of Residual-Free Transformers
Authors: Yiping Ji, Mahalakshmi Sabanayagam, Peyman Moghadam, Hemanth Saratchandran, Simon Lucey
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25880v2
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 12.5
Abstract: Large-scale transformer training and deployment are increasingly constrained by the transfer of activations, gradients, and optimizer states across accelerators. Low-bit quantization offers a natural remedy, but transformer activations are often heavy-tailed and outlier-dominated, making simple quantization highly lossy. We show that this difficulty is not only a property of the quantizer, but also of the architecture. Specifically, residual connections can drive transformer activations away from Gaussianity during training. Using controlled comparisons between residual and residual-free transformers, we demonstrate that this effect leads to substantially higher quantization error and accuracy degradation at low precision in residual models. We explain the phenomenon through an excess kurtosis analysis, showing that residual mixing can amplify non-Gaussianity, whereas dense mixing in residual-free models contracts non-Gaussianity. We then show that residual-free transformers can be made trainable using orthogonal initialization, spectral or second-order optimization, and depth-aware scaling of attention temperature. In language tasks, while there is a small drop in full precision performance, these models retain near-Gaussian activations and exhibit significantly improved robustness to low-bit quantization. Our results identify an accuracy--compressibility trade-off in transformer design and motivate architecture-level approaches to quantization-friendly foundation models.

4. Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Chenruo Liu, Zihan Wang, Yijun Dong, Qi Lei
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08414v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is commonly used in both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), often under similar optimizer settings. Yet, how the different dynamics of the two stages manifest in the low-rank increment that LoRA optimizes remains unclear. Tracking the singular spectrum and subspaces of LoRA updates on mathematical reasoning tasks, we find that RL concentrates the spectrum at every learning rate, while SFT expands it at moderate learning rates; moreover, RL rotates the singular subspaces much faster than SFT at small learning rates. Interpolating between SFT and RL one ingredient at a time, we pinpoint group-mean baseline subtraction as the source of both contrasts. A quadratic multi-index model updated via LoRA unveils the underlying mechanism: group-mean baseline subtraction tilts the balance between two competing forces, coherent fitting and gradient noise, toward spectral concentration and faster subspace rotation, while forcing the risk-favorable learning rate to be low. Guided by these dynamics, we tailor LoRA updates to each stage: in SFT, we flatten the spectrum of the full update to spread adaptation across singular directions, whereas in RL, we flatten the part of the update outside the current LoRA increment span to promote subspace rotation. Experiments on mathematical reasoning tasks across multiple models show that the respective configurations for SFT and RL yield the best performance among the compared methods in most evaluated settings.

5. Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout
Authors: Tushaar Gangavarapu, Gil Tabak, Clemens JS Schaefer
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Linear attention compresses context into a fixed-size key-value state for linear-time training and constant-memory inference. Gated delta-rule models decay this state and erase its prediction for an incoming key before writing a new value; Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) generalizes this with channel-wise decay and decoupled, full-rank erase and write gates. However, we show that state updates are far less sensitive to erasure than to writes: key activation and unit normalization concentrate key energy onto a sparse subset of channels, whereas value writes scale all channels, so the erase gate can be parameterized at lower rank. Furthermore, slow-decaying state rows accumulate much larger norms than fast rows, dominating unweighted readout and forcing stronger write-time erasure to suppress interference. Guided by this analysis, we introduce Muir, which fuses all gates into a sensitivity-allocated low-rank projection, re-weights state rows with a matched-filter readout gate, and reinvests the saved parameters in a wider feed-forward network. Because standard chunkwise algorithms become numerically unstable at the large chunk sizes needed to saturate wide matrix units, we design a parallel update for stable, efficient training. Across 166M-1.34B parameters, Muir lowers perplexity by 1.8-2.0% over GDN-2 and 2.5-3.6% over Transformer, raises commonsense accuracy by up to 1.9 points, and improves extractive recall over GDN-2. At 1.34B, Muir outperforms both baselines on multi-hop and multi-needle recall, extrapolates 2,560x from its 4K training length to 10M-token streams without perplexity drift, and maintains near-100% passkey retrieval to 1M tokens with static readout. Muir achieves these gains while matching GDN-2 throughput, reaching its 100B-token validation loss 1.2x faster, and training 2.7x faster than Transformer at 32K.
