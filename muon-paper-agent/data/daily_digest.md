1. A Margin Perspective on LoRA: Robustness to Catastrophic Forgetting and Adapter Merging (MaLoRA)
Authors: Ziqing Xu, Hancheng Min, Lachlan Ewen MacDonald, Salma Tarmoun, Enrique Mallada, Weijie Su
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14397v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 8.0
Abstract: Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is the de facto method for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large neural networks, achieving strong task adaptation with minimal trainable parameters and low optimization cost. Beyond this efficiency, practitioners have observed two striking properties: \textit{robustness to catastrophic forgetting} and the ability to \textit{merge independently trained adapters} into a single model that performs competitively across multiple tasks. These properties are central to continual and multi-task learning, yet remain poorly understood. In this work, we provide a theoretical explanation through a unified \textit{margin-based perspective}. We analyze LoRA in multiclass linear classification under a near-orthogonal task regime with $\ell_2$-regularization, and characterize the optimal LoRA adapter across regularization regimes. Our analysis shows that, in an intermediate regime of regularization parameters, the optimal adapter aligns with the \emph{max-margin solution} on the fine-tuning data. Building on this characterization, we derive two key consequences. First, we obtain closed-form expressions for the margins on pre-training and fine-tuning data, revealing a precise margin trade-off: the regularization parameter controls the balance between retention and adaptation. Second, we analyze adapter merging, proving that merged models achieve positive margin on each task and deriving optimal mixing coefficients that balance margins across tasks and maximize the margin over their union. These results lead to a simple, training-free merging rule, which we term \textit{margin-based LoRA merging} (MaLoRA). Experiments on modern architectures and real datasets validate our theoretical predictions, showing that MaLoRA matches or outperforms several adapter-merging baselines across a range of vision and language classification tasks.

2. Block-Sparse Gauss-Newton Optimization of Finite Basis Physics-Informed Neural Networks: Sparse Automatic Differentiation Approach
Authors: Denis Korolev, Adrian Hill, Jeeeun Lee
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13231v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 8.0
Abstract: In this work, we develop an efficient Gauss-Newton optimization approach for finite basis physics-informed neural networks (FBPINNs). The key ingredient is the overlapping domain decomposition architecture of FBPINNs, which localizes the influence of each subnetwork and induces block sparsity in both the residual Jacobian and the corresponding Gauss-Newton matrix. We characterize this structure and exploit it through sparse automatic differentiation, using graph coloring to partition structurally orthogonal Jacobian columns or rows. This reduces differentiation costs and memory requirements while preserving all structurally nonzero derivative information. The resulting compressed Jacobian representations allow us to assemble the Gauss-Newton system directly in block-sparse form, enabling the use of sparse direct solvers and preconditioned iterative methods. In this way, the otherwise expensive second-order optimization problem is reduced to scalable sparse linear algebra. We demonstrate the approach on multiscale PDEs, where accurate approximation requires rich neural network parametrizations and large numbers of collocation points. Numerical experiments show that our block-sparse Gauss-Newton method attains high accuracy and substantially accelerates optimization relative to first-order baselines while remaining computationally tractable for large problem sizes.

3. Spatial-Spectral Coupling as Attention: Scaling Shape Correspondence to High-Resolution Surfaces
Authors: Bence Demeter, Kilian Ehrenreich, Amelie Kleber, Ioana Oprisiu, Philipp Spohn, Christian Wachinger
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13870v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 9.0
Abstract: Establishing dense correspondence between high-resolution 3D shapes remains challenging due to the spatial--spectral coupling at the core of many functional-map methods, which grows quadratically with mesh resolution. We observe that this coupling is equivalent to a scaled dot-product attention formulation, with learned descriptors serving as queries and keys and spectral basis functions as values. This connection makes efficient attention implementations directly applicable to shape matching and an effective mechanism for scaling spatial-spectral coupling. We show that optimized quadratic attention implementations can scale modern deep functional map pipelines to large mesh sizes, improving both runtime and memory consumption, while sub-quadratic methods introduce errors, particularly for non-isometric shape pairs, where an exact coupling proves most necessary. These insights motivate \textit{Geo-FlashAttention}, which restricts the exact softmax to geometry-selected surface patches and achieves near-linear empirical scaling while preserving correspondence accuracy of exact implementations. Across six standard benchmarks and three functional-map pipelines, rigorous analysis shows that efficient couplings preserve correspondence accuracy. At $60k$ vertices, these insights reduce peak training memory from 106.08 to 5.67\,GiB, with Geo-FlashAttention being $1.5\times$ faster per training step than FlashAttention. This enables end-to-end learning for correspondence problems on large meshes such as cortical surfaces, where high resolution is required to preserve disease-relevant intricacies of the surface geometry. Project page available https://spatial-spectral-attention.github.io/

4. Pyrite: Building and Benchmarking Protein-Ligand Pose Ranking Agents
Authors: Megha Srivastava, Michael H. Lim, Allison M. Keys, Pranav Murugan, Nate Gruver
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14511v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Predicting how a ligand binds to its target is central to small-molecule drug discovery. Modern cofolding models can generate diverse candidate poses, but reliably ranking these candidates remains challenging. We introduce Pyrite, a pairwise pose-ranking benchmark comprising 1,500 pose pairs across diverse protein-ligand complexes. Expert chemists equipped with 3D visualization tools and physics-based scores perform near chance, highlighting the difficulty of interpreting available chemical signals. To investigate automated ranking, we introduce PyriteRep, a structured representation of molecular geometry, protein-ligand interactions, and physicochemical scores, and develop two LLM-based agents: PyriteReasoner, which directly compares poses through text-based reasoning, and PyriteFeaturizer, which generates executable code to construct features for logistic regression, optionally incorporating features derived from LLM subcalls. These agents achieve up to 67% and 74% accuracy, respectively, exceeding observed expert performance. LLM-guided feature discovery generally outperforms direct reasoning, while Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) improves open-weight models, enabling them to match or surpass closed-source frontier models in our evaluations. These results establish Pyrite as a challenging test of pose-ranking ability and highlight LLM-guided feature discovery as a promising approach to interpret and combine the outputs of computational chemistry tools.

5. Statistical Analysis of the Structure from Noise Phenomenon in Generalized Procrustes Problems
Authors: Jiayang Yin, Shuyang Ling
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13546v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 6.0
Abstract: In cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM), observations are highly noisy, and scientists often use reference templates to guide matching, alignment, and averaging to reconstruct the molecular structure. However, these procedures can produce an output resembling the template even when the observations contain only noise, a phenomenon known as Einstein from noise (EfN). This phenomenon has attracted attention in statistics, where simplified observation models involving group actions have been studied to understand the resulting model bias. Characterizing the bias in the full cryo-EM pipeline remains challenging. We study template matching and averaging in the generalized orthogonal Procrustes problem, which provides an analytically tractable setting involving continuous orthogonal group action and lower-dimensional projections of ordered point clouds. We show that, for pure Gaussian noise, the mean of the aligned-and-averaged estimator admits the same left and right singular-vector bases as the template but its singular values may be distorted. This identifies the subspace structure imposed by the template even in the absence of signal. We characterize the singular values and bound their distortion, establish finite-sample operator-norm bounds, and give sufficient sample sizes for the population mean to dominate random fluctuations. In fact, this bias still exists under weak signals: when the template rank is at least the observation dimension, the change from the pure-noise mean is $O(η^2)$ in signal amplitude $η$. Our results provide an explicit characterization of model bias in a registration problem motivated by cryo-EM and show why averaging more observations can stabilize structure imposed by the template without removing the bias.
