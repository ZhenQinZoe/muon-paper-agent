1. BiMuon: A Geometry-Aware Stochastic Bilevel Optimization Approach
Authors: Zhen Qin, Zhiyao Zhang, Yingbin Liang, Jia Liu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10188v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 24.5
Abstract: Stochastic bilevel optimization provides a general framework for many machine learning applications, such as hyperparameter learning, data reweighting, language model alignment, and actor-critic reinforcement learning. However, most existing bilevel optimization methods treat matrix-valued gradients as flattened vectors and perform optimization in the resulting vectorized space, ignoring the matrix structures common in modern neural networks. Moreover, the lower-level (LL) problem curvature can induce significant spectral imbalance in the upper-level (UL) geometry. Such limitations of existing bilevel optimization methods motivate us to develop BiMuon, a geometry-aware stochastic bilevel optimization method that integrates implicit hypergradient estimation in bilevel optimization with Muon-style orthogonalized updates at the UL. To our knowledge, BiMuon is the first stochastic BLO method with Muon-style orthogonalized matrix updates and an $O(ε^{-4})$ iteration-complexity guarantee, matching the best-known rates achieved by Muon-type methods based on matrix parameterizations. To improve computational and parallel efficiency for models with multiple matrix parameters, we further study BiMuon for blockwise diagonal matrix approximation and derive a relative stepsize allocation rule based on UL progress and blockwise LL sensitivity. We evaluate BiMuon on representation learning and blockwise bilevel optimization, providing empirical support for our methods.

2. DisMUSE: An Efficient Distributed Singular-Spectrum-Based Optimization Approach with Theoretical Convergence Guarantee
Authors: Zhen Qin, Zhiyao Zhang, Yingbin Liang, Jia Liu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10212v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 19.0
Abstract: In recent years, singular-spectrum-based optimizers have attracted increasing attention due to their ability to preserve the matrix structure of neural network gradients, thereby enabling geometry-aware optimization. However, most singular-spectrum-based (SSB) approaches remain centralized, limiting their use in distributed learning infrastructures that exploit massive parallelism. Distributed SSB optimization is still at an early stage, with open questions about convergence guarantees and communication efficiency. To fill this gap, we propose the Distributed Momentum-based Spectral Estimator (DisMUSE), an efficient distributed SSB optimization framework for both data-center and federated settings. Using Muon-style exact orthogonalized momentum as the spectral update, DisMUSE integrates a recursive variance-reduced momentum estimator and achieves an $O((KT)^{-1/3})$ convergence rate, where $K$ and $T$ denote the number of nodes and local iterations per node, respectively. This rate matches the best-known rates for variance-reduced nonconvex distributed optimization. Building on this variance-reduced framework, we study two extensions. First, our DisMUSE method with finite Newton-Schulz (NS) iterations preserves the same convergence order under a uniform normalized support-spectrum condition, up to a multiplicative factor. Second, we develop a communication-efficient variant based on low-rank (LR) momentum transmission and quantify the error it introduces to the stationarity bound, characterizing the trade-off between accuracy and communication cost.

3. Quantum Natural Muon: Spectral-Natural Optimization for Variational Quantum Circuits
Authors: Duong The Do, Duong Tung Nguyen
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10105v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 18.0
Abstract: Training parameterized quantum circuits requires optimization methods that account for two distinct structures: the information geometry of quantum states and the matrix or tensor structure induced by circuit parameterization. We introduce Quantum Natural Muon (QNM), a spectral natural-gradient framework that applies polar normalization to circuit-semantic parameter blocks after damped quantum Fisher preconditioning. This ordering is essential: quantum Fisher preconditioning and blockwise polar normalization do not commute in general, so QNM is not an order-free composition of quantum natural gradient (QNG) and Muon. We show that Frobenius-matched polar mixing preserves active singular subspaces while raising a spectral floor and improving active conditioning, and that the resulting direction remains a controlled perturbation of QNG. For its distance-aware realization (DA-QNM), we further establish alignment, descent, and nonconvex stationarity guarantees for the normalized update, together with perturbation bounds under gradient and QFIM estimation error. Experiments on variational quantum eigensolving, weighted QAOA-MaxCut, and supervised quantum classification isolate the roles of quantum geometry, circuit-semantic spectral structure, and radius control. The strongest gains arise when the spectral blocks match the circuit structure, supporting the central principle that quantum-state and structured spectral geometries provide complementary information for variational quantum optimization.

4. Spectral Rank and Step-Direction Dynamics in Language-Model Post-Training
Authors: Khurram Khalil, Khaza Anuarul Hoque
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11752v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Language-model post-training modifies weights through cumulative updates and successive displacements, yet existing studies report seemingly conflicting spectral properties: nearly full-rank updates and low stable-rank changes. We show these observations are compatible and characterize update directions during training. For singular values following $σ_i \propto i^{-α}$ with $0 < α\leq 1/2$, we prove that stable rank becomes negligible relative to fixed-energy rank as dimension increases. Full singular value decompositions of selected OLMo-2-7B updates confirm this separation: three proximal policy optimization (PPO) attention updates have stable ranks of 9.6-18.3 but require 532-1,222 singular components to capture 80% of their energy. We next compute consecutive checkpoint-displacement cosines from full-model inner products. Across nine publicly released reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) stages, all 131 adjacent pairs have negative cosines on their native checkpoint grids, including PPO and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) trajectories. Supervised fine-tuning comparisons across four model families show configuration-dependent alignment, including negative late-training cosines for Mistral and FP32 Llama. Finally, a controlled OLMo-2-1B study with three paired seeds and a 2,048-token response cap compares group-relative advantages against a learned critic with generalized advantage estimation (GAE). At matched 60-rollout windows, late-training mean cosines are +0.259 and +0.012, respectively; the paired critic-minus-group difference is -0.247 (seed-level 95% interval [-0.358, -0.137]). These findings distinguish spectral concentration, energy coverage, and trajectory direction, and demonstrate that the advantage system influences directional alignment under matched training conditions.

5. NearFormer: Linear-Size Sparse Attention via Randomized Near Neighbors
Authors: Ali Parviz, Alex Cloninger, Gal Mishne
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12186v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Graph transformers can capture long-range interactions, but their quadratic attention cost limits their use on large graphs. We introduce \emph{NearFormer}, a graph transformer whose global attention is restricted to a randomized near-neighbor (NN) graph. NearFormer uses an efficient randomized construction to connect each node to a small number of near neighbors from a broader neighborhood defined by a label-independent feature or spectral geometry. For a fixed retained budget \(k\), the resulting attention support has linear size \(\mathcal{O}(|E|+nk)\). The NN graph is built once by within-bin search and then held fixed. We use these edges as sparse global-attention supports, with optional learned attention biases, while preserving the original graph for local message passing. Experiments on long-range graph benchmarks and synthetic structural tasks show that NearFormer provides a favorable accuracy--scalability trade-off relative to local message passing, dense and sparse attention.
