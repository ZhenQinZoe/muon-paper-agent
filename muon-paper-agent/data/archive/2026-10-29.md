1. GTFormer: A Syntax-Free Graph Network Alternative to Transformers for 8192$\times$ Length Extrapolation in Linear Time
Authors: Elisabeth Fink
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37524v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: Self-attention mechanisms have established the Transformer as the dominant backbone across natural language processing, source code intelligence, and automated theorem proving. However, all-to-all pairwise token comparison enforces a quadratic $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ compute and memory barrier alongside severe softmax attention dilution as sequence length $N$ scales beyond the training horizon. Inspired by recent advances in algebraic graph learning (\textbf{WPNet}), this work introduces \textbf{GTFormer}, a syntax-free hierarchical Graph Neural Network (GNN) that replaces flat self-attention for general long-context sequence modeling. GTFormer organizes raw token streams into a bounded-degree hierarchy combining a balanced binary reduction tree ($\lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ depth), horizontal boundary-bridging shift edges, random expander shortcuts, multi-channel convex highway gating, and a top-down broadcast pass. Across eight long-context code, log, and multi-hop retrieval benchmarks trained exclusively on sequences of length $N \le 512$, GTFormer extrapolates zero-shot to $N = 4{,}194{,}304$ ($8192\times$) on a single commodity GPU in linear $\mathcal{O}(N)$ memory, maintaining near-perfect accuracy where standard and RoPE Transformers suffer from out-of-distribution attention collapse and out-of-memory exhaustion.

2. A note on data-efficient finetuning of vision foundation models
Authors: Niclas Hergenröther, Antonio Orvieto
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37510v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: Adapting foundation models to a new task within or beyond the training domain is the de facto standard for building specialized machine learning models. Recently, Visual Prompting (VP) has emerged as a promising technique for efficient knowledge transfer. As new foundation model families~(like Mamba) get introduced and VP pipelines such as AutoVP reach greater maturity, we find a growing need for a systematic evaluation of current approaches. In this work, we assess the performance of the latest models, comparing them to earlier architectures and alternative fine-tuning methods, to better understand the progress, challenges and opportunities in the field of efficient fine-tuning under resource limitations. Towards this goal, this paper provides a concise empirical overview of the interactions among foundation model families (Attention-, Convolution-, and Mamba-based) and transfer paradigms: VP, Linear Probing (LP), Full Fine-tuning (FFT) and Low Rank Adaptation (LoRA). Our work builds up on previous findings by broadening the selection of evaluated models and techniques while tuning hyperparameters. In the interest of delivering practical guidelines for the user, we also explore application of prevalent regularization techniques to boost performance in the context of VP.

3. SpecLST: Spectral-Enhanced Long-Short Term Spatio-Temporal Multimodal Modeling for Traffic Forecasting
Authors: Kenan Kang, Matthew M. Y. Kuo, Weihua Li
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36393v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.5
Abstract: Modeling multi-scale dynamics in urban road networks is essential for intelligent transportation systems but is often hindered by the computational cost of long-range historical context and the challenge of fusing heterogeneous modalities. Existing spatio-temporal frameworks face a trade-off between sequence length and memory overhead, while frequently neglecting the transient effects of exogenous factors such as road incidents and weather. To address these limitations, we propose SpecLST, a frequency-enhanced long-short-term spatio-temporal multimodal framework. SpecLST leverages a fast Fourier transform-based frequency-domain representation module to efficiently encode multi-week historical dependencies, bypassing the memory bottlenecks of standard attention mechanisms over raw long sequences. Heterogeneous context is integrated through attention-based fusion, followed by a sparse Mixture-of-Experts backbone with graph augmentation for spatio-temporal representation learning. Additionally, a frequency-enhanced residual head isolates high-frequency transients, improving sensitivity to abrupt traffic anomalies. At the 60-minute horizon, SpecLST achieves mean relative error reductions of 14.10% in MAE, 10.35% in RMSE, and 15.78% in MAPE across eight neural forecasting baselines and four datasets, with the dataset- and metric-specific average reductions ranging from 5.63% to 22.21%.

4. PerceptRL: Making Visual Perception Rewardable in MLLMs from Answers to Interactions
Authors: Tongkun Guan, Zhibo Yang, Mingkun Yang, Haocheng Wang, Wei Shen, Xiaokang Yang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37471v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Fine-grained visual perception remains a bottleneck during post-training of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), as perceptual errors can constrain gains from reasoning-focused optimization. Although image-caption pre-training improves perception, its influence can weaken as subsequent stages change data mixtures and objectives. Continuing caption supervision during post-training is challenging because subjective, information-dense descriptions complicate verifiable reward design. We present PerceptRL, which strengthens perception at two complementary levels. Perception RL uses a multi-agent pipeline to extract verified errors from dense image captions and convert the corresponding visual facts into QA pairs with fixed answer labels and rule-based rewards. Chain QA links correlated errors, while multiple-choice QA combines unrelated errors. A specialized route targets high-error domains with tailored verification, and a domain-agnostic general route covers errors beyond these domains. Perception Agentic RL addresses fine-grained and dense cases unresolved by single-turn Perception RL through multi-turn inspection with visual tools. We observe coordinate drift even in models trained to use tools, where incorrect crops or annotations distort evidence and propagate errors. Coordinate Injection corrects spatial operations in cold-start trajectories, while a grounding-aware process reward supervises intermediate spatial fidelity alongside final-answer correctness. Training proceeds through shared cold-start SFT, single-turn Perception RL, and multi-turn Perception Agentic RL. Across seven benchmarks under a shared tool-use protocol, PerceptRL achieves 71.6% average accuracy, surpassing the base model with the same tools and the strongest evaluated frontier VLM by 15.7 and 5.2 percentage points, respectively.

5. Kernel Performance Engineering under Physical Non-Idealities
Authors: Supun Kuruppu, Pranav Dangi, Wei Li, Zhenyu Bai, Tulika Mitra
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37466v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Physical non-idealities in integrated circuit (IC) design and fabrication are concealed by conventional hardware abstractions, yet they can limit kernel performance and obscure optimization opportunities. Using distributed shared memory (DSMEM) on NVIDIA H100 GPUs as a case study, we show how floorsweeping restricts hardware resources available for clustered execution, potentially offsetting the benefits of reduced data movement enabled by DSMEM. Guided by these insights, we develop a wave-aware clustering strategy that accounts for these resource constraints to improve kernel performance. Evaluations using a global-load-and-broadcast microbenchmark, GEMM, and the Hopper version of FlashAttention 4 demonstrate the performance implications of these constraints, with wave-aware clustering improving FlashAttention 4 throughput by up to 5.30% over its baseline. We further show that, under thermal design power (TDP) limits, reduced data movement enables higher Streaming Multiprocessor (SM) frequencies through Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS), explaining otherwise unexpected throughput improvements. Extending this analysis beyond GPUs, we show that power constraints also limit throughput scaling on Tenstorrent's Wormhole accelerator, highlighting the broader importance of accounting for physical non-idealities when analyzing and optimizing kernel performance.
