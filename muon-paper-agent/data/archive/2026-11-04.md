## Top Highlights

1. Theoretical Analysis of Muon on Transformers for Next-Token Prediction
Authors: Shengwei Wu, Yixuan Yang, Gao Huang, Song Li
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04465v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 17.5
Abstract: Muon has recently shown strong empirical performance in training Transformer-based large language models (LLMs), yet its optimization dynamics remain poorly understood from a theoretical perspective. In this paper, we analyze the training dynamics of Muon for next-token prediction (NTP) in Transformers. The training procedure consists of two phases: the preprocessing phase learns the output-value component, while the main phase learns the key-query component. For the preprocessing phase, we characterize the optimization trajectory exactly and show that the normalized spectral margin is optimal after each update. For the main phase, we establish nonasymptotic convergence rates for the training loss and the normalized margin gap under softmax attention. These results suggest that Muon's update rule can help models efficiently learn which tokens matter for prediction, offering a possible explanation for its strong performance in large language model training. The learned weight structure also yields a generalization guarantee for unseen contexts. Furthermore, we validate our theoretical insights with experiments on next-token prediction tasks.

2. Finite Spectral Depth Controls Gaussian Entrance Rank and Avoidance of the First Gradient-Flow Saddle
Authors: Yanlin Long
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05022v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 16.5
Abstract: Matrix factorization provides a simple model for studying the training dynamics of neural networks. Recent studies of gradient flow under small initialization have analyzed how trajectories escape from a neighborhood of the origin and approach their first nonzero stationary point. In this paper, we study continuous-time dynamics that applies a finite number of Newton-Schulz iterations to the normalized matrix gradient (Muon). We show that the number of Newton-Schulz steps can change the rank selected during the early stages of training and allow the resulting trajectory to bypass the first nonzero stationary point approached by gradient flow. Under an additional residual-inertia condition, we prove that bypass occurs for all sufficiently large finite Newton-Schulz depths and provide an explicit sufficient threshold. We also construct an example in which the bypass threshold is four.

3. ZEMA: confidence-calibrated signed descent with only one state
Authors: Ali Dadsetan, Domenic Rosati, Frank Rudzicz
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04987v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 10.5
Abstract: Recent work characterizes the Adam optimizer as sign descent modulated by gradient uncertainty: the update is the sign of the batch gradient, scaled by how strongly the batch supports that sign. We apply this view directly and introduce ZEMA (Z-score Evidence Momentum Adaptation), which measures that support with a per-coordinate $z$-score computed from the per-example gradients in the current batch, converts it into a sign probability, and smooths the resulting update with a single exponential moving average. Because the probability is formed before smoothing rather than after, ZEMA keeps one state per parameter instead of Adam's two, at a $4.6$ increase in per-step time; the per-example variance it requires can also be estimated from a small fraction of the batch. In fine-tuning and pretraining experiments, ZEMA is competitive with Adam, reduces the variability of the validation loss, and in some settings recovers the sign of the full-dataset gradient more consistently. We close with two directions this opens: extending confidence adaptation beyond the sign geometry, to methods such as Muon, and to differentially private training.

4. Sheaf-MPNN: A Library for Sheaf Neural Networks as Message Passing
Authors: Alessio Borgi, Mario Severino, Luke Braithwaite, Steve Azzolin, Rémi Bourgerie, Olga Zaghen
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04670v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Standard Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) implicitly assume that neighboring nodes share the same feature space, which contributes to oversmoothing and poor performance on heterophilic graphs. Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) remove this assumption by giving each node and edge its own vector space and learning restriction maps that transform features as they move between neighbors. These maps define a sheaf Laplacian that drives diffusion over the graph, but existing implementations often assemble it explicitly as a global sparse block matrix, which adds memory and computational overhead and can hinder scalability. In this paper, we introduce Sheaf-MPNN, a PyTorch Geometric library that reformulates sheaf diffusion as local incidence-wise transport and aggregation, eliminating the need to construct the global Laplacian. Sheaf-MPNN implements fifteen families of SNNs under a unified message-passing framework, each supporting both homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs, and directed and undirected settings, with diagonal, general, orthogonal, and low-rank restriction maps and attention-based predictors. We further provide a unified benchmarking suite covering node- and graph-level tasks, heterogeneous graph learning, and computational efficiency, enabling systematic and reproducible comparisons across architectures. We establish theoretical equivalence between these message-passing formulations and their original counterparts and identify the architectural mechanisms needed to preserve their propagation dynamics. This unified perspective provides a principled foundation for implementing, comparing, and extending sheaf-based architectures within standard graph learning frameworks. Source code is available at https://github.com/alessioborgi/sheaf-mpnn.

5. A simple adaptive accelerated optimistic dual averaging method for unconstrained convex optimization in $p$-norm geometry
Authors: Bowen Li, Danqing Zhou, Chuan He
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05334v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Efficient preconditioning plays an important role in large-scale first-order optimization. In this paper, we propose an adaptive accelerated optimistic dual averaging ($\text{A}^2$ODA) method for unconstrained convex optimization that adopts low-cost preconditioning based on $p$-norm geometry for $p\in[2,\infty]$. The method is line-search-free and employs a new stepsize selection technique based on an aggregated affine lower model. Its update structure is simple and unified across $p\in[2,\infty]$, with the case $p>2$ requiring no more elaborate updates than the Euclidean case $p=2$. Under a generalized local smoothness condition, we derive a convergence bound that separates the effects of constant and gap-dependent curvature, with the latter potentially yielding faster-decaying terms. In the special case of local smoothness, this bound recovers a nearly optimal convergence rate.
