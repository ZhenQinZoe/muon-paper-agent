1. SPARE: Fast Linear Attention Decoding with Compact Recurrent States
Authors: Ke Wan, Zaiyi Zheng, Chen Chen
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07552v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Linear attention reduces the quadratic sequence-length complexity of softmax attention by maintaining fixed-size recurrent states. However, these states scale quadratically with the representation dimension and require repeated data transfers during decoding, creating a substantial GPU memory bottleneck. Recent studies have revealed significant low-rank redundancy in recurrent states, suggesting opportunities for more efficient representations. We introduce SPARE (State Projection for Accelerated Recurrent Execution), a training-free method that exploits this redundancy through fixed low-dimensional subspaces, heterogeneous rank allocation, and lightweight write compensation. By performing recurrent reads and updates directly on compact states, SPARE reduces memory traffic without online decomposition or dense-state reconstruction. SPARE achieves 2.55x full-model decoding speedup, 7.49x lower recurrent-kernel DRAM traffic, and 3.31x lower peak GPU memory, while preserving better associative-retrieval accuracy than structured pruning at matched memory budgets.

2. ArtCrafter: Feed-Forward Generation of Articulated 3D Object with Analytic Joint Derivation
Authors: Minh Canh Tu, Quang-Binh Nguyen, Khoi Nguyen
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07658v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Articulated 3D assets are essential for robotic simulation and training, yet their generation from casual images poses a key challenge: recovering part geometry and kinematics that remain mutually consistent. We present ArtCrafter, a novel feed-forward generative model for controllable generation of articulated 3D objects from a pair of images, one depicting the object in the rest state and the other in the open state. At the core of ArtCrafter is a DiT-based generative model that jointly produces part-level meshes for both articulation states in a single forward pass. Inspired by Slot Attention, its denoising process operates on a $1+2N$ slot layout comprising one shared base slot and $N$ paired rest/open slots, encouraging each pair to bind to the same kinematic part. To ensure geometrically consistent and disjoint part generation, we introduce Triplet Attention to enforce cross-state geometric consistency between paired rest/open slots anchored through the shared base slot, and a repulsion loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rep}}$ on decoded part point clouds to prevent inter-part penetration throughout denoising. Given the resulting paired meshes, URDF joint parameters---type, axis, origin, and range---are derived analytically from the rigid transformation between each rest/open pair, providing an interpretable alternative to learned joint-parameter regression. Extensive experiments on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets demonstrate that ArtCrafter outperforms existing method under the same dual-state setting in geometry quality and articulation precision, while remaining competitive with methods operating under different input settings and paradigms.

3. Profile-aware diagnostics of discarded parameter-Hessian mass
Authors: Maxim Bolshim, Alexander Kugaevskikh
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01335v2
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Evaluating block-diagonal curvature models involves two questions: how much curvature they omit, and what a specific correction changes at a finite step. We measure the squared off-block fraction of the parameter Hessian against references for group sizes and the observed within-group curvature profile. In CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 and a skip-free counterpart, similar initial discarded fractions accompany different profiles. At saved trained ResNet-18 weights, changing from stage to layer groups moves estimated off-block mass from below to above the profile envelope; an approximate conditional screen resolves this comparison in all six seed/input cells, while some skip free comparisons remain unresolved. Separately, in saved ResNet-50/Stanford Cars states, we compare candidates formed from off-group Hessian action, the remaining model residual and their combination through the same Hessian-informed map. At unit scale off-group input alone raises native minibatch loss relative to the base preconditioner candidate in 17 of 18 state/geometry comparisons, yet the combined candidate has lower loss than the remainder-only candidate in nine of those cases; preferences vary with scale and correction-norm matching. The profiles help assess block models and alternative groupings, while same-state candidate comparisons test a particular correction without equating structural mass with training gain.

4. SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery and Optimization
Authors: Qile Jiang, George Em Karniadakis
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08483v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: LLM-guided evolutionary search uses AI agents to improve programs for mathematical and computational problems by iteratively refining solutions and combining high-scoring methods. However, the process is often slow because the LLM generates a long textual chain of thought for each round and must repeat this generation many times to maintain population diversity. We present SwarmEvolve, a framework in which hundreds of low-cost agents per round work in swarms, reason in latent space without decoding to text, and share information by passing KV caches. Each swarm studies the problem from a different angle. A novelty filter keeps the most distinct agent ideas by directly measuring the distances between the agents' hidden state vectors. Crossover of ideas happens naturally in latent space, as a summarizer agent receives the combined KV caches of all swarm members. On 21 problems in mathematical discovery and scientific computing, SwarmEvolve gets the highest average score and needs a median of only 3 to 8 minutes to reach the best scores that other state-of-the-art frameworks reach in 60 minutes. On several problems, SwarmEvolve with Qwen3 8B improves on the solutions that AlphaEvolve first found with frontier LLMs. Our work shows that agent swarms that communicate in latent space offer a fast, low-cost approach to program search for scientific problems.

5. T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models
Authors: Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen, Yingyan
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.
