1. LoopTitan: What Does Parameter Revisit Buy a Mixture-of-Experts?
Authors: Zichen Tian, Yudong Lu, Xuansheng Wu, Na Zou, Xia Hu, Guanchu Wang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34821v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 9.5
Abstract: Looped Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures revisit layer parameters across depths. This design increases effective depth without adding parameters. Recent looped MoE studies report substantial gains over the standard MoE at matched budgets (parameters, FLOPs and KV cache). However, the reported gains still confound parameter revisit with other design choices, e.g., network shape and residual scale. We ask, what does parameter revisit itself buy? Using matched non-revisit controls, we find that parameter revisit itself buys almost nothing. We argue that revisit only offers a freedom an MoE already has, i.e., trading experts for depth (more layers, fewer experts each) at fixed parameters. First, at matched budgets, we decompose the state-of-the-art looped recipe, with revisit last. A non-revisit control retains all the gain. Most comes from network shape, and the rest from residual scale. We prove this scale acts as a lower learning rate on revisited layers. This helps under AdamW's uneven layer step sizes, but disappears under Muon, which keeps them nearly equal. Second, beyond matched budgets, we evaluate revisit by adding compute at fixed parameters. One extra visit gains more than doubling the experts. Yet without revisit, a deeper standard MoE at equal parameters and compute obtains 90.2% of that gain under Muon. Overall, gains come from network shape, the optimizer and added compute, which a standard MoE can adopt. Accordingly, we propose LoopTitan-Base as a stronger MoE baseline. Under Muon, it matches the strongest looped recipe without revisit or residual scale. We open-source LoopTitan and release 17 pretrained models (including LoopTitan-Base and the control). Our experiment record is public as GitHub issues, with predictions pre-registered and the full exploration history kept. This is our Phase 1 technical report, laying the foundation for follow-up studies.

2. Do We Really Need an LLM for Weight-Space Programming? A Deterministic Forward-Only Paradigm and Its Capability Boundaries
Authors: Lin Yao
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34612v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 8.0
Abstract: Recent work on Weight-Space Programming uses LLMs to edit neural weights with forward passes only, conflating LLM scripting with the underlying algebra of forward-only editing. We show that for core structured editing regimes, an LLM optimizer is unnecessary, and introduce Forward Weight-Space Programming (FWSP): a deterministic pipeline combining probing, localization, stratified operators (O1-O4), automatic blind routing (83.3% vs. 33.3% for a 27B LLM with the same tool library), and paired verification with zero additional per-token computation. For reasoning LLMs, we propose Protected FisherGate, a closed-form covariance-whitened readout edit orthogonal to normal-reasoning states: on DeepSeek-R1-0528-8B, a single frozen math-calibrated patch achieves 84.2% HumanEval pass@1 (1K budget; 75.4 +/- 9.4% across 3/5 Stage-4-passing subsets), beating 512-token-reserved budget forcing by +14.6 pp (-23.4% tokens) while transferring across HF-to-vLLM backends on full GSM8K and MATH-500. Across five modalities and thirteen models (50K to 27B dense and FP8 MoE), we further prove and verify two operator boundaries, subspace entanglement and frozen representation span, where FWSP-localized LoRA bridges forward-only and gradient adaptation.

3. Repairing the Trustworthiness of Distilled Language Models
Authors: Kumar Shubham, Devendra Vyas, Vidushi Agarwal, Jayin Khanna, Prathosh AP
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34831v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Knowledge distillation enables the deployment of large language models through smaller student models, but the compression process can introduce regressions in trustworthiness even when downstream performance is preserved. Repairing such failures is challenging because the original distillation corpus is often unavailable, while direct preference optimization may alter capabilities inherited from the teacher. We propose a targeted repair framework based on the proximal Bregman response function (PBRF) that addresses both limitations. We estimate the curvature required for PBRF using a surrogate proxy dataset, removing dependence on the original transfer corpus, and extend the framework to preference-based supervision through an odds-ratio objective. This enables the use of preferred and dispreferred response pairs while constraining changes to the distilled model. Our results show that the proposed approach improves targeted trustworthiness behaviors for both pre-training and post-training models while largely preserving downstream performance, providing a practical alternative to re-distillation or unconstrained preference optimization. Our experiment results show that across different distilled models, the trustworthy regression can be improved by up to 8% with less than 1% decrement in perplexity.

4. Global Convergence of Second-Order Methods for Physics-Informed Neural Networks in the Overparameterized Limit
Authors: Konstantin Riedl, Justin Sirignano, Konstantinos Spiliopoulos
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34452v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Solving high-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs) constitutes a core task in many engineering and scientific disciplines. Physics-informed neural networks and the deep Galerkin method effectively leverage machine learning models to approximate solutions of known PDEs by minimizing residuals corresponding to the PDE operator alongside external constraints. Although second-order optimizers are becoming increasingly popular for training neural networks (NNs) to solve PDEs and empirically outperform their first-order counterparts in both speed and accuracy, the convergence theory for training such models is essentially confined to first-order methods. In this paper, we close this gap and develop a convergence analysis for the regularized Newton and Gauss-Newton methods. As the number of hidden units tends to infinity, the training dynamics converge in probability, with explicit rates, to the solution of a deterministic limit equation involving a Newton neural tangent kernel operator associated with the PDE. Although the limiting kernel operator admits no spectral gap, we prove that, in the overparameterized limit, the PDE residual vanishes as training proceeds, and, under mild assumptions, the NN converges to the PDE solution. We further show that, provided a sufficiently small regularization parameter, the projection of the residual onto any fixed number of leading eigenfunctions decays exponentially fast, demonstrating that this hyperparameter acts as a spectral filter: it lifts the convergence rates of high-frequency components toward those of low-frequency ones, thereby mitigating the spectral bias inherent in gradient descent. Mathematical challenges arise from the implicit parameter update involving a linear system whose dimension increases with the NN width and, for Newton's method, a potentially indefinite Hessian, as well as from the PDE operator entering the kernel.

5. Learning to Be Truncated: Preparing LLM Checkpoints for SVD Compression
Authors: Chenyue Fang, Danila Mishin, Babak Falsafi
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34667v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 6.5
Abstract: Compression based on singular value decomposition (SVD) often requires subsequent fine-tuning or weight correction to mitigate performance degradation. Budget-specific optimization must be repeated for different compression ratios. We introduce LLift, which prepares a pretrained model for SVD compression across ratios without further fine-tuning. LLift fine-tunes a low-rank approximation at a single ratio using the original model as a teacher. We merge the learned updates into the original weights while preserving the complementary components excluded from the training forward pass. We apply SVD to the prepared model's target matrices and reuse the decompositions at different truncation ranks. On LLaMA-7B, we demonstrate accuracy gains across three compression ratios using a single prepared model. The gains extend across model families, model sizes, and SVD compression operators. We analyze gradients and an early training update to provide local evidence that updates learned at one ratio can benefit compression at other ratios. These results show that LLift can improve compression across deployment budgets without fine-tuning each compressed model separately.
