1. Muon Attains $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-3})$ Iteration Complexity under Shuffling
Authors: Kun Huang, Shi Pu, Karl Henrik Johansson
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18124v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 17.0
Abstract: Muon is a stochastic first-order optimizer for matrix-valued parameters. It maintains an exponential moving average of stochastic gradients in a momentum buffer with coefficient $β$, and orthogonalizes the buffer to obtain the update direction. Muon has been reported to accelerate and stabilize the training of large language models. In practice, it is often used with shuffling (sampling without replacement), where each epoch visits the components of a finite-sum objective in a permuted order. However, its iteration complexity in this setting remains open. We analyze Muon under shuffling, with the momentum buffer orthogonalized by the polar map. For smooth nonconvex finite-sum problems, we establish an $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-3})$ iteration complexity for finding an $\varepsilon$-stationary point. This guarantee holds for every sequence of permutations, covering random reshuffling, shuffle once, and the incremental gradient method. This complexity matches that of stochastic gradient descent under shuffling in its dependence on $\varepsilon$, and improves on Muon's $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})$ complexity under sampling with replacement. Our guarantee requires a problem-dependent $β$ that approaches one as the number of components grows. In contrast, no fixed $β$ guarantees convergence to stationarity for every number of components. We show this by constructing a family of smooth convex problems on which, for any fixed $β\in[0,1)$, Muon remains bounded away from stationarity once the number of components is sufficiently large. This failure occurs for every sequence of permutations and persists even with small stepsizes. These results suggest two practical tuning rules: if the training loss stops decreasing even after reducing the stepsize, increase $β$; when increasing $β$, reduce the stepsize accordingly.

2. Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining
Authors: Md Rifat Arefin, Ravid Shwartz-Ziv, Ernie Chang, Chinnadhurai Sankar, Rylan Conway, Aristide Baratin
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17876v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 12.0
Abstract: A temporary optimization intervention can increase validation loss when it ends yet reduce it at the final training budget. We demonstrate this with temporary weight decay in Muon-trained decoder-only Transformers with 162M, 405M, 1.4B, and 3.4B parameters. For models up to 1.4B parameters, moving a fixed-duration decay window from early to late training reverses its final effect. At 162M, changing window duration alters final loss even at approximately matched total shrinkage. Most strikingly, a schedule selected at 162M and transferred without retuning to 1.4B is 0.26 nats worse than no decay when removed, overtakes it only after 73% of training, and ultimately finishes 0.028 nats better while also outperforming the best sampled constant-decay baseline. Early decay leaves a persistent weight-norm deficit, which keeps relative steps larger after the intervention ends. An approximate norm-dynamics recurrence captures the slow convergence of these relative steps, and fixed-norm replays of the measured trajectories reproduce the performance ordering and a similar loss difference. At 162M and 405M, norm-reset experiments show that smaller weight norms at the end of the decay window explain most of the final loss improvement. A separate Gram intervention exhibits related post-removal norm and relative-step dynamics. These results show that temporary optimization schedules can have delayed effects long after removal and should therefore be evaluated at the intended final training budget, not only at removal.

3. Token2Tile: Token-Wise Sparse Attention with Tile Execution through a Low-Rank Space for Video DiTs
Authors: Xuecheng Wu, Aichen Feng, Jinyan Chen, Qingjie Zhao, Julien Lai
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17930v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Attention is essential for modeling spatio-temporal dependencies in video diffusion transformers, but its quadratic cost makes it a major inference bottleneck. Its high sparsity offers an opportunity to accelerate generation by focusing computation on important interactions. However, accuracy and execution favor different granularities: each query attends to its own key tokens, whereas GPU kernels work on tiles. We present Token2Tile, a training-free sparse attention operator that combines per-query scoring and a per-query residual with tile execution. An online low-rank space, computed directly from queries and keys to preserve attention logits, makes clustering, scoring, and residual approximation efficient. Token2Tile unites query-specific selections to allocate exact work across query clusters, then packs the selected keys into GPU tiles. On MiniMax-H3 and Wan2.1-14B, Token2Tile delivers 1.48-1.54x end-to-end speedup over dense FlashAttention-3 with VBench scores within 0.5 points of dense. At comparable speeds on H3, it improves PSNR over SOL and SubBlock by 5.5-6.8 dB; on Wan, it exceeds SOL by 4.6-4.8 dB while running faster.

4. Any Channel Count, One Token Stream: Incorporating Spatial Audio Understanding and Reasoning into Large Audio-Language Models
Authors: Yuhuan You, Wenxiang Guo, Zhifang Guo, Yuxuan Wang, Jin Xu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18112v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Audio phase relations encode source locations, yet existing large audio-language models (LALM) reduce audio to single-channel content to stabilise general audio understanding, leaving spatial information for direction, distance, and source-relation questions unused. We present SpheraAudio, which accepts recordings with any channel count -- mono, stereo, First-Order Ambisonics (FOA), or higher -- and maps them into a single token stream. A spatial encoder turns complex multichannel spectra into spatial tokens interleaved with audio tokens at corresponding temporal positions. Spatial Multimodal RoPE (SMRoPE) makes each token's modality identity and temporal slot visible to rotary attention without altering the language model's original RoPE. Training runs in three stages -- spatial-encoder pretraining, projector alignment, and joint supervised-and-reinforcement learning -- progressing from geometric supervision to language-based spatial decisions, with answers trained as explicit spatial chain-of-thought: step-by-step geometric reasoning over the heard scene. Current benchmarks tie questions to a fixed channel-count format and score answers without spatial reasoning evidence. We introduce SpheraQA, which spans mono, stereo, and FOA recordings and organises questions hierarchically from single-attribute perception through cross-source relations to multi-step reasoning, scored programmatically against simulated scene geometry and real-recording annotations with step-by-step solution references. Across SpheraQA and public spatial-audio benchmarks, SpheraAudio sets a new performance standard for spatial audio understanding. It supports high-precision localisation, tracking, and reasoning over static and moving sound sources while preserving strong general-audio capabilities. We will release code, SpheraQA, and evaluation metrics to support reproducibility.

5. EcoVGGT: Pinned-Key Inference for Efficient and Streamable Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction from Long Videos
Authors: Hongsen Cao, Shanxin Yuan, Mona Jaber, Ahmed Sayed
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18099v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Feed-forward multi-view transformers such as VGGT recover the cameras and geometry of a video in one pass, but the cost of their cross-frame attention grows quadratically with the frame count, and their accuracy drops on long videos. Most remedies approximate attention over all frames within a budget or select a few keys by their content. We hypothesize that the keys of a long video can be chosen without looking at its content, and our experiments on VGGT and $π^3$ support it: a few dozen evenly spread frames can serve as the keys of every frame. We present EcoVGGT, a training-free inference framework whose keys form a Pinned Key Set of 31 such frames. A pin pass stores their tokens in a Scene Cache, and the other frames read it in independent batches that reproduce joint inference with these keys exactly, at a constant cost per frame. An Online Stream updates the pinned frames during recording. Six middle global blocks align the frames and form a reusable VGGT module: the Alignment Score reads their attention to flag failures, and Alignment Refocusing reduces them by fine-tuning only these blocks (3.6 GPU hours). EcoVGGT ($π^3$) matches the success rate of $π^3$ on 1,000-frame ScanNet videos at 13.8 times its speed. With refocusing, EcoVGGT (VGGT) raises the success rate of VGGT from 70% to 82% at 8.5 times its speed on 500 frames. EcoVGGT reconstructs 4,000 frames on one GPU, and EcoVGGT ($π^3$) streams 1,000 frames at 18.6 frames per second with the lowest online absolute trajectory error.
