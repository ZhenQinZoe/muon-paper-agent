1. Federated Learning of Car Aerodynamics Surrogates with Spectral Optimizers
Authors: Arman Bolatov, Nikolay Spitsyn, Daniil Medyakov, Vladimir Aletov, Dmitry Bylinkin, Martin Takáč
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25300v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 14.5
Abstract: Neural surrogates predict the aerodynamic forces on a car in seconds, compared with hours for a CFD simulation, but training them requires thousands of CFD simulations of different car designs, which car manufacturers keep proprietary. Federated learning allows several manufacturers to train a shared surrogate without exchanging data. We study which federated optimizer brings such a surrogate closest to one trained on pooled data. On DrivAerNet++, we train a Transolver surrogate across six clients that each hold cars of a single body type, and we compare three spectral optimizers, which orthogonalize the update of each weight matrix (FedMuon and two methods we propose, FedMARS-M and FedOLion), with three non-spectral ones (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD-Adam and FedLion), all tuned with the same protocol. FedMuon and FedMARS-M reach a drag error within 2% of centralized training with Muon, using two to four local epochs per round. The non-spectral methods perform best with sixteen local epochs, the largest number we tested, and still have a 17 to 37% higher drag error than FedMARS-M. We relate this gap to the low stable rank of the weight gradients, for which an orthogonalized step yields about twice the first-order decrease of a gradient step of the same spectral norm. Code: https://github.com/armanbolatov/fedmars_drivaernet

2. Variational analysis of determinantal varieties
Authors: Yan Yang, Bin Gao, Ya-xiang Yuan
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22613v3
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 8.0
Abstract: Determinantal varieties---the sets of bounded-rank matrices or tensors---have attracted growing interest in low-rank optimization. The tangent cone to low-rank sets is widely studied and underpins a range of geometric methods. The second-order geometry, which encodes curvature information, is more intricate. In this work, we develop a unified framework to derive explicit formulas for both first- and second-order tangent sets to various low-rank sets, including low-rank matrices, tensors, symmetric matrices, and positive semidefinite matrices. The framework also accommodates the intersection of a low-rank set and another set satisfying some assumptions, thereby yielding a tangent intersection rule. Through the lens of tangent sets, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition under which a nonsmooth problem and its smooth parameterization share equivalent second-order stationary points. As applications, we exploit tangent sets to characterize optimality conditions for low-rank optimization and prove that verifying second-order optimality is NP-hard. Moreover, for low-rank semidefinite programs, the Burer--Monteiro parameterization maps fourth-order stationary points of the parameterized problem to second-order stationary points of the original problem. In a separate line of analysis, we investigate variational geometry of the graph of the normal cone to matrix varieties, deriving the explicit Bouligand tangent cone, Fréchet and Mordukhovich normal cones to the graph. These results are further applied to develop optimality conditions for low-rank bilevel programs.

3. Interpretable Prototype-Conditioned Adaptation for Weather Forecasting
Authors: Zhixuan Li, Hao Jiang, Chen Hui, Kai Geng, Weisi Lin
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25848v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 6.5
Abstract: Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to interpret. Failing to distinguish local evolution can lead to similar forecasts for locations that develop differently, increasing prediction error. This motivates explicit references for distinguishing and interpreting local patterns. Prototypes provide explicit pattern references, but sample- or task-level conditioning leaves open how to adapt to distinct locations and variable groups. To deal with this problem, we design ProtoCast, which uses group-specific prototype banks to adapt local features while accounting for cross-variable dependencies. Specifically, (1) we combine each group's historical features and temporal change with forecast context from cross-variable attention for evolution-aware prototype matching. (2) Sparse mixtures of prototype-specific low-rank operators tailor corrections to each group and location. A gated attention block then exchanges the corrected features across groups before prediction. Each group's correction decomposes into explicit prototype contributions before this fusion. (3) We jointly train the forecasting network and operators from scratch and refit each group's prototypes before every training epoch. Slot tracking preserves prototype-operator associations, making each prototype's contribution to local corrections more interpretable. Experiments on GlobalForecast and TyphoonTracking show improved forecasting accuracy, while prototype deletion links local feature contributions to changes in prediction error. This work advances the development of more accurate and interpretable data-driven weather forecasting models. Project page: https://zhixuanli.github.io/projects/li2026protocast

4. Frequency-Synchronized Boundary Coupling for Training-Free Multi-Prompt Long Video Generation
Authors: Yipan Xu, Shuyong Gao, Jiyuan Fu, Keliang Yin, Jiayu Chen, Qianyu Guo
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26098v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.5
Abstract: Text-to-video generation has made remarkable progress with diffusion models and transformer-based video architectures, yet generating coherent long videos from evolving textual descriptions remains challenging. Training long-video models from scratch requires substantial computation and large-scale multi-event data, while training-free multi-prompt generation must activate new prompt semantics without breaking temporal continuity. We identify a frequency-agnostic boundary coupling problem in existing transition strategies: weak coupling causes abrupt changes, flicker, and background jumps, whereas overly strong or coarse coupling may over-preserve source semantics and produce ghosting or duplicated subjects around prompt boundaries. To address this problem, we propose FreqSync, a training-free framework that formulates prompt transitions as frequency-selective boundary coupling. FreqSync combines Frequency-Synchronized Source-Conditioned Attention (FS-SCA) with Residual-Clipped Spectral Stitching (RCSS), enabling adjacent segments to share stable low- and mid-frequency structure and motion cues while keeping high-frequency prompt-specific details target-dominant under explicit residual constraints. Extensive experiments show that FreqSync achieves state-of-the-art transition smoothness and prompt alignment while maintaining strong perceptual video quality, demonstrating its effectiveness for coherent multi-prompt long video generation.

5. Temporal Contrastive Alignment of EEG and Naturalistic Video Only Beats Linear Decoding On In-Sample Stimuli
Authors: Yuanyuan Yao, Simon Geirnaert, Alexander Bertrand
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26258v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 6.5
Abstract: Neural tracking, the temporal alignment between a continuous stimulus and the elicited brain response, enables decoding of attention from electroencephalography (EEG), yet its application to naturalistic video remains largely unexplored and mainly relies on linear canonical correlation analysis (CCA). We investigate whether a temporal contrastive model, pairing pretrained VideoMAE features with a lightweight EEG encoder trained on a correlation-based InfoNCE objective, can strengthen EEG-video alignment. Benchmarked against CCA under three generalization regimes, the model outperforms CCA across subjects (leave-one-subject-out), matches it within the stimulus manifold (leave-video-segment-out), and falls behind CCA when extrapolating to entirely novel videos (leave-one-video-out). Because this advantage appears only for seen stimuli, we probe its basis: a sham control shows that a high accuracy does not certify genuine alignment, as a synthesized correspondence fit to the seen stimuli can reproduce it without genuine temporal alignment in the training pairs. This does not preclude practical use: on a downstream attention-decoding task with seen stimuli, the model remains better than CCA since the decoding only requires a strong statistical coupling, regardless of whether that coupling is physiologically meaningful. Using a group-averaged EEG reference, we further show that genuine, generalizable coupling is nonetheless learned, only buried in the noise of single-subject EEG. These results indicate that progress hinges on data scale, diversity, and single-subject denoising.
