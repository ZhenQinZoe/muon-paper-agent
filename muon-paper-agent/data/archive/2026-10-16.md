1. When Is Muon Optimal? Radial Fragility and Minimax Geometry
Authors: Ao Xu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21549v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 22.0
Abstract: Muon replaces a matrix gradient by an approximate polar factor and has shown strong empirical performance in neural-network training. The Isotropic Curvature Model (ICM) provides a curvature-based explanation for this update by asking when gradient orthogonalization minimizes a one-step local-loss surrogate. We investigate the stability of this explanation when the fixed input radius of the ICM is replaced by a general radial law. We introduce the Random-Radius Isotropic Curvature Model (RR-ICM) and the Polar-Factor Admissibility Set, which encodes exact scaled-polar optimality as a geometric condition on the positive polar factor of the gradient. We derive an exact characterization of this set for arbitrary radial laws and show that all nonscalar admissible responses arise from radial atoms aligned with curvature kinks. Consequently, every atomless radial law exhibits zero-capacity rigidity: exact scaled-polar optimality is possible only when the gradient is already a scaled column isometry. To connect the curvature-based framework with the established spectral geometry of Muon, we introduce the Minimax RR-ICM by replacing directional averaging with worst-case optimization over probability laws on the sphere. We prove that every nonzero global minimizer lies on the polar ray of the gradient, while its scale is determined by a one-dimensional convex problem. The corresponding admissibility geometry is a positive-semidefinite trace slab, in sharp contrast to the spherical-zonoid geometry of the RR-ICM. Together, these results expose the radial fragility of average-curvature explanations and provide a new minimax variational characterization of Muon's polar direction.

2. When Does Muon Actually Win? A Paired Data-, Time-, and Tuning-Budget Benchmark
Authors: Mushir Akhtar, Hazem N. Nounou, Majdi Mansouri, Mohamed N. Nounou
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21555v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 15.0
Abstract: Muon's gains in data efficiency motivate evaluating its performance under explicit data, time, and tuning budgets. On a single A100 GPU, we compare a reference single-device Muon implementation with AdamW across 17.7M- and 33.6M-parameter causal language models and 2.70M-parameter vision transformers. A common 23-candidate development budget selects configurations evaluated with five matched seeds, full validation trajectories, optimizer-step timing, and a frozen one-shot test. Muon reduces validation-loss area under the curve by 3.25--22.44\% at equal data and lowers test loss in all 20 primary pairs. In this configuration, Muon improves time-normalized loss area} by 19.68--22.31\% on vision, while AdamW is better by 3.38--6.96\% on language. The full Muon optimizer call, including auxiliary AdamW updates, consumes 56.1--69.6\% of training time, compared with 6.1--9.1\% for the AdamW baseline. Muon also has higher expected regret under restricted access to these candidate pools. These results characterize small models with reference kernels; their wall-clock ordering does not establish a ranking for larger models or optimized implementations.

3. Amplitude-Only FFN Intervention for Tool-Structured LLM Inference: Method, Gated Evaluation Protocol, and Cross-Model Empirical Results
Authors: Sheng Xu, Zhen Chen, Junhua Wang, Boyuan Huang, Ke Jia, Jiadun Zhu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11183v4
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 10.0
Abstract: We study Amplitude Gating (AG), an inference-time intervention that masks feed-forward network (FFN) activation coordinates while keeping pretrained weights fixed. Six SwiGLU sites separate shared and gate-only inputs, expanded gate/value channels, and the FFN output. Magnitude-ranked entropy masks and a Newton-Schulz windowed ranking variant provide alternative forward paths without retraining. The corrected September collection covers three Qwen checkpoints and eight text/tool endpoints. Under frozen local text scorers, the displayed Qwen3.5-9B non-NS configurations improve two function-call endpoints by +2.61 and +2.67 percentage points (pp), and static ToolBench in/out-domain endpoints by +1.64 and +2.82 pp. Comparing the lower historical/September AG mean with the higher baseline mean leaves positive margins on these routes. This supports robustness to the two observed executions, rather than formal statistical significance. JSON gains are small and displayed L-Eval effects are negative. These endpoints measure lexical or answer/action-name agreement rather than executed-tool success. Protocol-specific offline selector and Per-Sample Fix-Harm Evaluation (PFHE) analyses complement the fixed-route results. Current experimental data do not establish a significant random-forest (RF) benefit; further experiments are needed. AG provides a task-dependent intervention surface, with causal mechanisms, equal-budget advantages, repeated-run uncertainty, and serving cost still open.

4. Does On-Policy Distillation Need to Complete Every Rollout?
Authors: Zhengxin Zhang, Maosen Tang, Ning Wang, Changhao Li, Jingren Zhou, Bo Dai
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21337v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 9.0
Abstract: On-policy distillation (OPD) effectively transfers capabilities from a teacher to a student through feedback on student-generated rollouts. However, generating complete student rollouts and computing teacher feedback for every token make OPD computationally expensive. We introduce Stochastic Truncated On-Policy Distillation (STOPD), which accelerates OPD by randomly truncating student rollouts and correcting the resulting sampling bias with blockwise inverse-probability weighting (IPW). At each training step, we sample a stopping position for each rollout before generation and let the student generate until that position or natural termination. We obtain teacher feedback on every generated token and use blockwise IPW to compensate for the lower probability of retaining later tokens while controlling the weights. We prove that this weighting preserves Vanilla OPD's gradient sum in expectation and derive one-step update-error bounds for SGD, Adam, and Muon. We evaluate STOPD across multiple teacher-student model pairs, task domains, and optimizers. STOPD reduces generated tokens by 25-48% while maintaining accuracy comparable to Vanilla OPD, achieving 1.3-2.2x training speedups.

5. Towards Continual Learning: Mitigating Forgetting With Subspace Tuning
Authors: Arnav Chavan, Kusha Sareen, Rishabh Tiwari, Abhranil Chandra, Johan Obando-Ceron, Kurt Keutzer
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22293v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 8.5
Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) acquire new skills through fine-tuning, but it can destroy capabilities they already have: catastrophic forgetting remains a central obstacle to continual learning in LLMs. Recent works connect forgetting to how far, and along which directions, fine-tuning moves the weights. On-policy methods such as reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation avoid updating the principal singular directions of weight matrices and thus forget less. In contrast, SFT concentrates updates along these directions and is susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. In this work, we introduce Subspace Tuning (SST), which restricts each weight update to the bottom singular subspace, eliminating changes along the principal directions. SST is optimizer-agnostic and composes well with fine-tuning recipes including SFT and self-distillation. SST combines tight subspace protection, dense gradient estimation, and a simple refresh mechanism, enabling stronger learning than prior orthogonal fine-tuning methods. With these mechanisms, protecting as much as 99\% of the weight subspace substantially improves retention while preserving strong target-task performance. This behavior strengthens with model and data size and nearly eliminates forgetting in the large-model regime. SST also keeps the model close to its base distribution, inducing $2$--$4\times$ less KL drift than the baseline recipes. Overall, SST advances the learning--forgetting Pareto frontier, making it a practical default for fine-tuning pre-trained language models.
