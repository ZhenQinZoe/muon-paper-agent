1. Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon-AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap
Authors: Catherine Li, Jingchu Gai, Andrej Risteski, Jacob Mitchell Springer, Aditi Raghunathan
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03633v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 15.5
Abstract: Muon has recently emerged as a promising optimizer for pretraining large language models, offering a speedup over AdamW in minimizing pretraining loss. However, it remains unclear whether Muon's advantage persists after fine-tuning. In this paper, we study the downstream behavior of Muon and AdamW and uncover a surprising trend: beyond a sufficiently large pretraining data budget, AdamW outperforms Muon after fine-tuning. Moreover, as model size increases, AdamW overtakes Muon at progressively smaller pretraining token-parameter ratios. To explain this reversal, we view fine-tuning as a perturbation of the outputs and introduce the margin-to-spread ratio, which measures how far the correct logit lies above the wrong ones relative to how widely the wrong logits are spread. In language models, Muon has the larger margin-to-spread ratio at small data budgets and AdamW at large ones, mirroring the fine-tuning trend. In a linear associative memory, we prove that after one update the margin-to-spread ratio of gradient descent gains on that of Muon as the data budget grows, and simulations show that this trend persists after extended training.

2. ProLand: A New Infeasible Manifold Optimization Method for LoRA Fine-Tuning
Authors: Chenyu Zhou, Yuxuan Zeng, Qi Deng, Dongdong Ge, Yinyu Ye
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00236v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 15.0
Abstract: Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) enables parameter-efficient fine-tuning but can suffer from redundant basis directions. Constraining the basis factor to the Stiefel manifold addresses this redundancy through orthonormality. Landing methods allow temporarily infeasible iterates to explore a neighborhood of the manifold while progressively restoring orthogonality without per-step retractions. To improve optimization efficiency while retaining this flexibility, we introduce ProLand (Product-space Landing), a retraction-free optimizer that combines coordinated factor updates with Muon-style spectral directions. By designing the updates in the space of the represented weight increment, ProLand separates objective optimization from orthogonality restoration, supporting efficient feasibility recovery. This coordinated construction also enables Muon-style directions to guide objective optimization while preserving that separation. Experiments cover four GLUE validation tasks on DeBERTa-v3-base and seven language benchmarks on Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Moonlight-16B-A3B across multiple adapter ranks. Across these 11 tasks, ProLand achieves the highest average score among the compared methods in every tested encoder and decoder model-rank configuration.

3. Tune the Baseline First: Most of Muon's Scaled Weight Decay Gain Is a Tuning Gap, but Its Small Real Advantage Grows With Model Size
Authors: Esther Xin
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02529v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 13.0
Abstract: Scaled weight decay for Muon (Muon-SW) has been reported to train language models more efficiently than constant decoupled weight decay, but such comparisons usually reuse one learning-rate (LR) and weight-decay (WD) setting for both methods. We re-run the comparison under the MAI-Base-2 protocol: LR and WD are tuned jointly through their product for each method, on a source-scaled mixture-of-experts (MoE) scaling ladder (1.9M to 15M active parameters) that holds tokens per active parameter and data repetition fixed across sizes. We find: (1) Most of the gain is a tuning gap. At the shared setting Muon-SW wins by 0.035 bits per byte (BPB), a 1.52x compute saving; after both methods are tuned, the gap is 0.0026 BPB (95% CI [0.0018, 0.0034]), a 1.03x saving, so about 90% of the advantage disappears. (2) The small advantage that remains is real: at the smallest size, all four paired seeds favour Muon-SW at the tuned settings. (3) That advantage grows with model size: across an 8x range of active parameters, the tuned gap rises from 0.0036 to 0.0051 BPB while the untuned gap shrinks from 0.035 to 0.024, so the surviving share rises from about 10% to 21%. (4) The best weight decay moves with scale: the optimal LRWD product grows for both methods, faster for Muon-SW (ratio 1.40 to 1.91), and a law fitted on the two smallest sizes overshoots the third by 1.3-1.5x, costing up to 0.002 BPB. The whole study runs on one NVIDIA T4 GPU; we release the code and all run results.

4. Do Orthogonality Constraints Help Language-Model Training Beyond Sphere Optimization?
Authors: Kai Hu, Li Zhang, Weichen Yu, Matt Fredrikson, Haoqi Hu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00593v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 13.0
Abstract: A growing body of work reports benefits from orthogonality constraints in fine-tuning large language models (LLMs). These results raise an open question: do orthogonality constraints also help LLM pre-training? We conduct a systematic empirical study of decoder-only LLM pre-training with models ranging from 360M to 3.1B parameters, trained on nearly 200B tokens. We examine fourteen variants of orthogonality training: some constrain the weight matrix itself, while others use orthogonal factors to reparameterize it. Because a strictly orthogonal square matrix loses half of its degrees of freedom, we also study block-wise orthogonality, where every $d$ rows form a row-orthonormal (Stiefel) matrix and the block size $d$ is a hyperparameter. Among block sizes ranging from 1 to the shorter side of the matrix, no $d>1$ used throughout the network reliably outperforms $d=1$, and large blocks are clearly worse; the one placement that helps, symmetric positive-definite factors on the query and key projections, loses its small gain as models grow. At $d=1$, the constraint reduces to sphere optimization, with each row direction on its own unit sphere, which by itself already improves on tuned Muon. The same two findings hold for a diffusion transformer trained on ImageNet, and after fine-tuning, downstream accuracy does not separate the parameterizations. Our conclusion is that, across the realizations we tried, orthogonality between rows does not help language-model pre-training beyond sphere optimization. Since this appears to conflict with several prior results, we re-examine five prior methods in pre-training and fine-tuning that report orthogonality improvements. In each case the reported gain is attributable to numerical precision, training horizon, optimizer realization, or per-row norm control rather than to the orthogonality the methods impose.

5. BISECT: Bilevel Spectral Decomposition for Low-Rank Continual Learning
Authors: Tom Saliencro, Wen-Hsuan Liu, Rohan Desai, Amara Okonkwo, Maya Lindqvist
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03574v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 12.0
Abstract: Exemplar-free continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers increasingly relies on Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), yet prevailing methods govern the low-rank update space through strict subspace isolation. By projecting each incoming task into the approximate null space of historical token activations, isolation-based learners equate any cross-task feature overlap with catastrophic interference. We show that this orthogonality premise induces a structural bottleneck we term \emph{spectral starvation}: when down-projection bases are locked before task optimization, the gradient norm reaching the trainable up-projection is upper-bounded by the energy of current-task features captured by the chosen basis. Because natural visual tasks share low- and mid-level covariance structure, historical null tails contain barely $5.5\%$ of incoming task variance, starving new-class plasticity while blocking positive consolidation along shared directions. We propose BISECT (Bilevel Spectral Decomposition), a continual LoRA framework that partitions a fixed rank-$r$ budget into two orthogonal functional blocks: an invariant carrier spanned by the dominant modes of the joint historical-and-current Gram matrix, and a novelty complement extracted via generalized Rayleigh whitening followed by orthogonal complement projection. After single-task training, BISECT folds both blocks back into the frozen backbone using a closed-form precision gate that scales each rank-1 invariant atom by its current-to-joint Rayleigh ratio while preserving novelty atoms at unit gain. Across eight incremental settings on ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and DomainNet, BISECT outperforms prompt, adapter, and LoRA baselines with zero extra inference parameters or FLOPs, and remains complementary to sparse analytic expansion.
