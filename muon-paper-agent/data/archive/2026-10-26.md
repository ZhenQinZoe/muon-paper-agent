## Top Highlights

1. Sharpness-aware Orthogonalized Momentum Algorithms
Authors: Geetika, Shivani Singh Chauhan, Gauranshi Gupta, Anant Jain, Kaushik Kalyanaraman, Bapi Chatterjee
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32465v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 13.0
Abstract: Adversarial sharpness penalty per iteration of a base optimizer, known as sharpness-aware minimization (SAM), is an accepted technique to improve generalization in deep learning. Recently, Muon has emerged as a dominant optimization algorithm for training large language models (LLMs). It achieves lower sample complexity for heavy-tailed gradient updates - a phenomenon common in LLM training. However, sharpness penalties with Muon updates are yet largely unexplored in the literature. Moreover, the behavior of SAM under heavy-tailed noise is unknown both theoretically and empirically. A potential reason for this gap is the additional cost of extra gradient computation per iteration for penalty perturbations in SAM, which becomes prohibitive for LLMs. This paper introduces Sharpness-aware Orthogonalized Momentum Algorithm (SOMA) to combine a computationally efficient and theoretically grounded perturbation scheme with the Muon algorithm. The key idea is to use stale (purely) stochastic gradient noise for adversarial perturbations. Theoretically, we show that SOMA's sample complexity is invariant to the perturbation direction. We provide explorations and insights into SOMA's robustness to heavy-tailed gradient noise. We extensively explore SOMA's efficacy in (a) mitigating quality degradation due to post-training quantization, and (b) catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning of LLMs.

2. Fast and Scalable Influence Estimation in the Independent Cascade Model: a unified framework
Authors: Matic Požar, Miklós Krész
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32502v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Computing diffusion processes on networks is fundamental to applications including influence maximization, epidemic modeling, information propagation, and diffusion inference. The Independent Cascade (IC) model is one of the most widely used models of progressive diffusion, yet exact computation of its activation probabilities is #P-hard, while Monte Carlo simulation becomes costly when accurate estimates are required repeatedly. Despite its central role, analytical approximation of the IC diffusion function has received comparatively little systematic attention relative to the extensive literature on influence-maximization algorithms. We present a comprehensive study of analytical approximations for the IC model. We review a wide range of existing approaches and unify them through a common framework based on temporal representation, retained propagation information, and aggregation rule. This perspective exposes connections between previously distinct approximation families and motivates several new methods. We further conduct, to our knowledge, the first large-scale cross-family comparison of analytical IC approximations for node-level marginal activation probabilities. Our benchmark spans real-world and synthetic networks with systematically varied topology, seed-set size, edge-probability model, and diffusion strength. The proposed second-order dynamic message-passing method achieves the lowest pooled median error and the lowest dataset- or graph-family-level median error on every network considered. The results reveal systematic differences between approximation families, identify regimes in which higher-order corrections are most beneficial, and establish analytical IC approximation as a rich accuracy-efficiency design space rather than a collection of isolated methods.

3. Video-MemComp: Taylor-Guided Sensitivity-Aware Memory Management for Streaming Video Understanding
Authors: Yonghui Niu, Yuhao Ji, Chang Nie, Jiao Zhang, Yilong Li, Junlan Feng
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32784v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: The rapid growth of video data has created an urgent need for efficient video understanding systems that can handle long and continuous visual inputs, posing significant challenges for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) due to severe memory bottlenecks. Moreover, existing compression methods often overlook the explosive accumulation of approximation errors. To address this issue, we propose Video-MemComp, a training-free and sensitivity-aware context management framework for memory-constrained streaming video understanding. Our method theoretically decomposes compression error into orthogonal local approximation error and layer-wise structural sensitivity via a first-order Taylor expansion. To minimize local approximation error, we perform token aggregation in a pre-RoPE safe zone with an attention-conserving logit bias, mitigating geometric distortions caused by RoPE rotations. To manage layer-wise structural sensitivity, we dynamically allocate the KV cache budget based on offline gradient priors and online context entropy, prioritizing high-sensitivity layers. Video-MemComp outperforms or achieves performance comparable to full-cache and state-of-the-art compression methods on benchmarks including MLVU, VideoMME, and EgoSchema. Notably, it improves the overall accuracy of Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 62.3% to 64.1% while reducing the KV cache to an equivalent of 6,000 tokens per layer, saving over 90% of KV memory.

4. RADIANT: Object-Centric Feed-Forward Global Illumination
Authors: Sarah Zhang, Zhiyang Dou, Minghao Guo, Benjamin Tod Jones, Wojciech Matusik
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32075v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Feed-forward neural renderers synthesize global illumination across novel 3D scenes without per-scene optimization or runtime ray tracing. Existing transformer-based renderers tokenize individual triangles, so self-attention scales quadratically with mesh resolution. Yet macroscopic radiative transport is governed by the underlying surface geometry and object arrangements rather than a particular mesh discretization; finer tessellation can thus inflate token counts without changing the underlying transport physics, motivating a discretization-agnostic and object-centric representation. We introduce RADIANT (RADiance-based Inter-Actor Neural Transformer), an object-centric architecture for feed-forward global illumination from point inputs. By representing each object with a compact budget of 32 surface-patch tokens, RADIANT decouples attention sequence length from polygon counts. In a single network, an inter-object scene transformer models 3D radiative exchange and a view decoder renders images at target camera poses. To capture light transport without materializing O(N^2) bias matrices, invariant form-factor attention embeds geometric distances and surface orientations directly into query-key dot products as SE(3)-invariant inner products weighted by learned area and material gains. For primary visibility without mesh connectivity, camera rays cross-attend scene tokens guided by depth-tested point splats in a shared spherical frame. Across a 1.03M-scene benchmark under matched model capacity, RADIANT reaches 25 dB tone-mapped PSNR in 5x fewer training steps than RenderFormer, and achieves 32.2 dB at 600k steps (+3.3 dB over RenderFormer and +7.6 dB over RenderFormer-V2). We further demonstrate the scalability of RADIANT, which completes a 512x512 forward pass of a 500-object scene in 0.83 s, with computational cost scaling with the number of objects rather than polygons.

5. UniAction: Unified Action Conditioning for World Models
Authors: Bowen Xue, Zizhang Li, Hong-Xing Yu, Jiajun Wu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32977v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Actions that condition world models are highly heterogeneous, and thus existing action-conditioned world models are typically built for individual action types, fragmenting already limited action-annotated data across separate models. We introduce UniAction, a unified action-conditioning framework that represents any action as a sparse set of action elements, each with a discrete semantic identity and a continuous spatiotemporal grounding coordinate. By mapping these coordinates directly into the pretrained video model's native positional space, UniAction allows heterogeneous actions to condition a shared world model within the same spatiotemporal structure. We further introduce cardinality-calibrated attention to address the bias from differing action representation granularities. We evaluate UniAction on four representative action forms: force, camera control, hand pose, and robot pose. UniAction outperforms conditioning alternatives in action following, and a single jointly trained model surpasses the action-specific experts, showing that heterogeneous action data benefit from unified model and joint training.
