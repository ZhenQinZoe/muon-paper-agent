## Top Highlights

1. EnergyCal: Taming Loss Spikes in Muon-Family Optimizers for Sparse MoE Training
Authors: Masato Fujitake
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37062v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 20.5
Abstract: Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) language-model training can exhibit large loss spikes with Muon-family optimizers. We introduce EnergyCal, a lightweight rule that attenuates routed-expert updates according to the normalized squared Frobenius norm of their finite-step Newton-Schulz directions. EnergyCal leaves the spectral transform unchanged and adds no persistent optimizer state. We evaluate it on a 4B-parameter MoE model. Against a development-selected, lower-learning-rate MuonH recipe, EnergyCal-MuonH reduces mean thresholded loss-spike burden by 99.51% and final geometric-mean perplexity by 4.06%, with a modest throughput cost. With plain Muon at matched nominal learning rate and weight decay, the reduction in mean spike burden averages 95.98% across the two tested exponents, accompanied by small endpoint improvements. These findings support stabilization both with and without Hyperball in the evaluated setting.

2. Sharpness-Aware Muon: Matrix-Aware Optimization with Momentum-Guided Perturbations
Authors: Zhiwei Zhai, Wenjing Yan, Ying-Jun Angela Zhang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37225v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 17.5
Abstract: Muon is a matrix-aware optimizer that combines momentum with orthogonalization to produce structured update directions with strong empirical behavior. However, it only shapes the geometry of the update itself and does not explicitly control neighborhood worst-case loss or bias training toward flat regions, which are often linked to stronger generalization. To address this limitation, we introduce the sharpness-aware formulation of Muon. We first develop \textsc{MuSAM}, which incorporates Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)-style perturbation into Muon's orthogonalized update and establishes a principled bridge between sharpness-aware training and matrix-aware optimization. We then show that this formulation faces an efficiency--stability tension: constructing the perturbation requires an extra gradient evaluation, while the perturbation itself is generated from a single minibatch gradient and is therefore noisy. To resolve this issue, we propose \textsc{MoMuSAM}, which turns Muon's momentum from an update variable into a sharpness-probing signal. By using normalized momentum as the perturbation direction, \textsc{MoMuSAM} removes the extra clean-gradient computation and replaces the single-batch perturbation proxy with a temporally aggregated one. We prove that both \textsc{MuSAM} and \textsc{MoMuSAM} attain the same asymptotic first-order stationarity order, and further show that \textsc{MoMuSAM} yields a more stable stochastic linearization than \textsc{MuSAM}. Experiments on standard benchmarks demonstrate that sharpness awareness consistently improves Muon.

3. MuonGS: Pretraining LLMs with Relaxed Weight-Norm Control
Authors: Athanasios Glentis, Dawei Li, Mingyi Hong
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37647v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 15.5
Abstract: Weight decay and explicit weight-norm constraints improve optimizer performance in language model pretraining, yet a unified understanding of why such control helps and how much is needed remains incomplete. We analyze norm control in Muon over the entire training trajectory using the \emph{accumulated effective learning rate}, $C_T=\sum_{t<T}η_t/\|W_t\|_F$, where $η_t$ is the nominal learning rate and $η_t/\|W_t\|_F$ is the effective learning rate relative to the weight norm. For bounded update directions, accumulation over any training interval bounds relative weight displacement. We show that vanilla Muon can accumulate only logarithmically, potentially limiting exploration and helping explain its slower empirical progress, whereas MuonW and MuonH guarantee at least linear accumulation under the stated conditions. Going beyond bounded-norm control, we show that sufficiently slow norm growth guarantees faster accumulation than the logarithmic worst case, opening up less restrictive optimizer designs. Based on this insight, we propose MuonGS, which uses Gram--Schmidt (GS) projection to remove the radial component of each Muon update without fixing the weight norm. With bounded update directions and a constant nominal learning rate, we prove that the weight norm grows at most proportionally to $\sqrt{t}$ after $t$ steps. Consequently, MuonGS guarantees $C_T=Ω(\sqrt{T})$, with accumulation sustained even in later training. Further, for smooth scale-invariant objectives we establish its $O(T^{-1/2})$ stationarity guarantee. In pretraining experiments with 160M- and 400M-parameter dense models as well as a 135M active- and 1B total- parameter MoE model, MuonGS achieves lower validation perplexity than MuonH at long training horizons. In particular, for the dense model it achieves an estimated token-equivalent speedup up to about $15\%$ at matched validation loss to MuonH.

4. Multi-State Linear Attention with Generalized Second-Order Routing
Authors: Hee Seung Hwang, Sanghyuk Chun, Xindi Wu, Olga Russakovsky, Zhiwei Deng
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37645v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 5.5
Abstract: Self-attention plays a key role in long-sequence modeling, but its quadratic time complexity fundamentally limits the scaling of Large Language Models. Recently, Multi-State Linear Attention (MSLA) mechanisms have been proposed to make sequence modeling more efficient by splitting the context into segments and compressing each in its own memory state. MSLA aggregates the information extracted from each memory state using only the mean alignment between a query and each segment's keys, ignoring how the alignment is distributed within the segment. In order to model the relevance of a segment to a query more comprehensively and incorporate the variance of the alignment in the aggregation, we propose MERLIN (MomEnt Routing for multi-state LINear attention), a method that tracks the first and second moments of each segment's keys. Using the moments of keys, a query can recover both the mean and the variance of its alignment with each segment to perform a more content-aware aggregation of per-state readouts. Experiments on multi-state variants of Gated DeltaNet-1.3B and Mamba-2-780M show that MERLIN consistently outperforms existing aggregation methods on various long-context tasks. In particular, MERLIN outperforms baselines on RULER needle-in-a-haystack and long-context QA (+8.3% for Gated DeltaNet-1.3B and +7.0% for Mamba-2-780M) and diverse subtasks in LongBench (+13.5% and +12.0%). It even outperforms baselines that consume 4x more memory. MERLIN provides a simple and general routing mechanism for improving long-context capabilities of MSLA models.

5. MoI-Bench: A Benchmark for Image Generation and Editing on Mobile Devices
Authors: Kailai Feng, Haoxiang Rao, Yuxiang Wei, Bo Chen, Junsong Li, Yang Pan
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37599v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 6.5
Abstract: Developing on-device image models requires considering practical user needs, task types and device resource constraints. However, existing benchmarks provide limited guidance on identifying capability gaps between on-device models and server-side models. We introduce MoI-Bench, a benchmark grounded in mobile user needs, comprising 64 subtasks and 1150 samples. It defines 16 operations, such as removal and replacement, and eight visual target categories covering content such as portraits and text. These form two orthogonal axes whose applicable combinations define specific tasks, such as removing facial makeup. To better guide model improvement, we further propose MoI-Eval, which introduces task-specific criteria covering instruction following, visual quality and detail preservation. We further construct sample-level evaluation questions and aggregate their scores into overall quality and capability profiles along the operation, target and criteria axes. We evaluate representative on-device and server-side models and measure mobile deployment costs. Results show that this decoupled Operation x Target design provides finer-grained guidance for improving compact models. For example, it highlights the need to prioritize specific capability gaps (e.g., text-replacement) over broad application-level goals (e.g., improving text editing). Unlike conventional benchmarks that rank models using a single score, we introduce a two-dimensional Quality-Deployability capability map to assess models for practical mobile use. The map shows that current on-device models can achieve efficient generation or editing but expanding their task coverage deserves greater attention than further improving deployment efficiency. Further progress requires improving editing quality, particularly identity preservation and text rendering, while broadening task coverage.
