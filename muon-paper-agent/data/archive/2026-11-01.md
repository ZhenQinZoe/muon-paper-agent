1. How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference
Authors: Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, Shruti Bhosale, Mike Lewis, Maryam Fazel-Zarandi
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 3.5
Abstract: Looping in transformer models reuses weights by repeatedly applying the same transformer, or parts of it, making the model deeper without increasing its parameter count. Whether looping is useful depends on which constraint dominates: model size, training compute, or autoregressive inference efficiency. We study these trade-offs jointly in dense decoder-only transformers trained from scratch, varying which parts of the model are looped, how many loops are applied, and how information is passed between loops. Our experiments span models with up to 1B parameters, trained on up to 155B tokens with up to $1.07\times10^{21}$ FLOPs and 32 loops. We find that looping the full transformer generally gives the strongest quality at a fixed parameter budget, whereas looping a middle block of layers gives a better return when training FLOPs are constrained. Adding loops is not uniformly beneficial: gains eventually saturate, reverse, or become unstable. This motivates asking whether forcing every loop to use the same weights limits the benefit of deeper looping. We address this by adding small loop-specific low-rank adapters that improve quality, with larger gains as more loops are added. Looped transformers also suffer a decoding slowdown proportional to the number of loops. Parallel Loop Transformers address this by passing hidden states between loops across adjacent token positions, allowing loop stages to run concurrently. However, in our experiments the original formulation does not consistently retain the quality gains of ordinary looping. We find that preserving the current-token embedding and separately normalizing it and the previous-token hidden state before combining them recovers strong performance across model scales and training budgets. Together, these results show that effective looping requires matching the design to the resource constraint and inference setting.

2. Attention, Dynamic Wave and Hybrid Sequence Mixers in Language Models
Authors: Dmytro Babarytskyi, Oleksandr Babarytskyi
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39854v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for sequence modeling, but scaling to long contexts is constrained by the quadratic cost of attention, motivating sub-quadratic recurrent alternatives. This paper formalizes a Dynamic Wave recurrent layer, based on input-dependent exponential damping and cosine phase modulation, and evaluates three 10.94M-parameter configurations (Pure Transformer, Pure Wave, and Hybrid Wave-Attention) on TinyStories under perplexity, latency, throughput and peak VRAM profiling up to 4096 tokens. The Hybrid configuration reaches the best perplexity, 5.95 on the full held-out split against 6.93 for the Transformer and 7.26 for Pure Wave. The efficiency picture, however, is governed by implementation rather than by architecture. Against an unfused attention baseline, Pure Wave uses 78.7% less peak VRAM at a 4096-token context (1404.3 MB vs. 6601.6 MB); against a fused scaled-dot-product attention kernel the same comparison yields no advantage at all (1404.3 MB vs. 1403.6 MB), and in training mode Pure Wave is 8.3% more expensive than the Transformer. Likewise, hoisting elementwise terms out of the recurrence loop speeds the layer up 2.53-fold with no custom kernel, reducing its latency gap to attention from 2.48-fold to 1.12-fold. A hybrid stack is found to inherit the cost of its more expensive mixer rather than the advantage of its cheaper one, its peak VRAM matching the Pure Transformer to within 0.1%. A closed-form memory model reproduces these measurements and predicts the context length beyond which attention overtakes the vocabulary projection. The findings indicate that reported efficiency gains for recurrent attention replacements must be stated relative to a named attention backend and a named implementation, and that hybridization is best justified on quality grounds.

3. Cross-Encoder Anchor Refinement for Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection
Authors: Kaisong Ji, Ziyang Xu, Chang Bian, Yu Zhou, Wenyu Liu, Xinggang Wang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39727v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Zero-shot anomaly detection aims to transfer anomaly discrimination from auxiliary training domains to unseen categories while localizing subtle defects. A key challenge is to construct discriminative references that combine contextual representations with complementary local evidence. We present Cross-Encoder Anchor Refinement (CEAR), which connects two visual encoding paths for the same image at the anchor stage, before normal and anomalous prototypes are constructed. An independent convolutional encoder pretrained for industrial product recognition builds an image-specific memory shared across selected vision transformer (ViT) layers. Spatial anchors read from ViT patch features query this memory through cross-attention and undergo residual refinement. Image-conditioned normal and anomalous queries then gate anchor aggregation to construct prototypes for anomaly scoring. Across 13 industrial and medical datasets, CEAR achieves the best performance in 57 of 68 evaluations spanning image-level detection and pixel-level localization. Notably, CEAR surpasses all other state-of-the-art methods in 41 of 44 pixel-level comparisons. Across 11 localization datasets, mean gains in pixel-level maximum F1 score and average precision are 4.45 and 3.79 percentage points, respectively. This study shows how cross-encoder interaction at the anchor stage can integrate complementary visual representations for fine-grained anomaly localization across domains.

4. AB-Attention: Accurate Block Attention for Accelerating Video Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Bin Xu, Mo Zhang, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Xinfeng Yang, Yixing Ma, Wei Wang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39663v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Video diffusion transformers generate high-quality videos, but the quadratic cost of 3D full attention dominates their inference. Dynamic block-sparse attention reduces this cost by computing exact attention only on key/value blocks selected from current activations. However, its fidelity degrades under aggressive sparsity: blocks are scored from coarse summaries or formed by costly global clustering, pruned blocks vanish from the softmax, and whole tiles are kept or dropped even when only a few of their keys matter. To address these limitations, we propose Accurate Block Attention (AB-Attention), a training-free method that accurately approximates dense attention at high sparsity. Its Intra-Block Clustering Selector (IBCS) estimates block importance faithfully yet cheaply by clustering queries only within each query block, without permuting tokens. Sampling with Inclusion-Probability Weighting (SIP) then samples blocks in proportion to these estimates and reweights them by inverse inclusion probability, so that one pass estimates dense attention rather than truncating it. Finally, its kernel gathers key/value blocks much smaller than a tile into full tensor-core tiles, refining selection at no extra tensor-core cost. On Wan2.1 and HunyuanVideo, AB-Attention skips 89--92\% of attention, whereas the baselines compute 1.8--3.6$\times$ as much, yet it still improves PSNR by up to 4.4~dB over the strongest baseline and achieves up to 2.23$\times$ end-to-end speedup. Our code is available at https://github.com/InfRec-Cardinal/AB-Attention.

5. Look at Where You Think: Localization-Head Alignment for Fine-Grained Perception in Thinking VLMs
Authors: Yuan Wang, Wei Wu, Hongning Wang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39649v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Thinking vision-language models reason for thousands of tokens, yet still miss small details in high-resolution images. Zooming methods pay for extra inference passes, and outcome rewards never tell the model where to look at. In Qwen3.5-4B, we find that the bottleneck is selection, not seeing: a few attention heads, which we call localization heads, pick the evidence out of thousands of visual tokens; ablating four of them costs 13.2 points more than ablating four random heads, and their focus fades as the reasoning grows. We therefore propose Localization-Head Alignment (LHA), which makes attention a training signal: whenever the reasoning states a visual fact, these heads learn to look at the focal region rather than the background, as an auxiliary loss in both SFT and RL training. On benchmarks of V*, HR-Bench 4K and TreeBench, LHA lifts Qwen3.5-4B from 74.1% to 76.6% accuracy, the best single-pass 4B result we evaluate, ahead of multi-pass zooming and released 7B models. Alignment alone adds 1.8 points over otherwise identical training while shortening reasoning by 20%, and the gains carry over to Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
