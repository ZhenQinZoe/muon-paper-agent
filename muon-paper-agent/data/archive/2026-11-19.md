1. Equal Steps, Unequal Curvature: Where Muon's Update Goes Wrong
Authors: Biswa Sengupta, Jinhua Wang, Léo Maxime Brunswic
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24644v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 20.5
Abstract: Muon updates each hidden weight matrix along the polar factor of its momentum, the steepest-descent direction under the spectral norm. That choice fixes two allocations without consulting the loss: every singular direction of the momentum receives the same step, and in a non-square matrix each neuron's step is set by its leverage score. Whether they are good is a question about curvature, which steepest-descent arguments do not answer; we measure it. Using held-out gradients and Hessian-vector products, we decompose Muon's step by singular-value band and by neuron group and compute each group's second-order optimal multiplier. In a 124M-parameter decoder trained on FineWeb-Edu, the multipliers rise monotonically from the top singular directions to the tail: Muon over-steps the high-curvature top of its spectrum. We prove in Lean when such a diagnostic licenses an intervention at Muon's own update norm. On the measured curvature, the criterion predicts, and held-out losses confirm, that moving step length off the top helps mid-training and early in the cooldown, but not at its end. We then test interventions under per-step norm matching, tuned baselines and paired seeds. Orthogonalizing Q, K and V separately rather than as one fused matrix lowers the loss by 0.0250 nats and accounts for all of the gain of block-wise variants. A matmul-only, rotation-equivariant spectral tilt $σ^{-1/4}$ applied in the cooldown lowers the loss of this stronger baseline by a further 0.0120 nats, additively with NorMuon (0.0158 nats together, three seeds; the baseline needs about 4.3% more tokens to match), and by 0.0188 nats at 355M parameters (three seeds). Energy-matched Schatten-1 weight decay adds a further 0.0052 nats. Geometrically principled changes that do not act on the measured miscalibration, namely exact polar factors, steepest-descent input whitening and top-heavy decay, do not help.

2. Do Frontier Model Ingredients Add Up? A Replicated Factorial Study of Small Language Models
Authors: Biswa Sengupta
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24540v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 11.0
Abstract: Building a small language model requires choosing which published improvements to combine. We compare five changes in the same model, using the same data and training budget. We test selected combinations and repeat each one with a second random seed. Mixture-of-experts layers, Muon optimisation and a Gated DeltaNet hybrid give the largest gains. Extra normalisation also helps, while an attention gate with a matched parameter budget adds little on average. The gains mostly combine, but small interactions between changes appear again in the repeated runs. Repetition also reveals benefits that an initial statistical estimate treated as noise. These findings apply to our small-model training setting; the expert models also have greater total capacity. The practical lesson is to test promising components together and repeat the comparisons before deciding which ones to keep.

3. QuantAtlas-OM: Taming Neural Spectral Bias in Long-Horizon Spatiotemporal Ocean Dynamics
Authors: Hao Wu, Yuan Gao, Tong Wu, Fan Xu, Fan Zhang, Jinhan Li
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24124v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Autoregressive neural networks exhibit spectral bias in long-horizon spatiotemporal prediction. As rollouts progress, high-frequency details decay, blurring the predicted fields and destabilizing long-range forecasts. Ocean dynamics offer a particularly stringent test because kinetic energy is concentrated in mesoscale eddies and fronts spanning tens of kilometers. This degradation stems from coupling large-scale phase evolution and small-scale energy retention in a shared feature space. Evolution on a coarse grid smooths out high-wavenumber eddies, whereas evolution on a fine grid amplifies small-scale phase errors and triggers rollout instability. We introduce QuantAtlas-OM (QuantAtlas Ocean Model), a scale-spectral decoupling framework that confines deep temporal evolution to a low-wavenumber latent manifold for long-term stability while routing full-resolution eddy and front residuals through an outer bypass to the decoder. Across physical regimes, the evolution core pairs non-local attention with resolution-invariant dilated spatial gating. Across three western boundary currents, QuantAtlas-OM retains $43.7\%$ to $86.5\%$ of mesoscale spectral energy along the $k^{-3}$ cascade at Day $120$, sustains anomaly correlation coefficients of $0.81$ to $0.88$ at Day $60$, and reaches $0.66$ at Day $120$ in the Kuroshio ($2.5\times$ to $7.7\times$ the best baselines). Trained at $0.25^\circ$, QuantAtlas-OM transfers zero-shot to a global $0.125^\circ$ grid ($0.81$ Day-$10$ spatial correlation) and leads global $0.25^\circ$, $23$-level 3D ocean simulations ($120$ days) and coupled ocean-atmosphere forecasts ($60$ days), ranking first on $15$ of $16$ metrics and cutting $60$- to $120$-day salinity RMSE by $13\%$ to $27\%$ across global ocean basins.

4. TRoPE: Topology-aware Relative Positional Encoding for 4D Generation
Authors: Chengzhuo Gui, Zhenglin Zhou, Fan Ma, Zongxin Yang, Yi Yang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23933v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 7.0
Abstract: Video-to-4D mesh generation has made substantial progress in recovering dynamic 3D geometry and motion from monocular videos. However, commonly used feed-forward approaches remain prone to sticking artifacts, where geodesically distant vertices move together incorrectly when they become spatially close. We attribute this failure to cross-part motion leakage: spatially nearby yet structurally distant vertices may retrieve similar motion information when motion decoding fails to distinguish spatial proximity from mesh connectivity. To address this issue, we introduce TRoPE, a topology-aware relative positional encoding that makes mesh connectivity explicit in the interaction between vertex queries and mesh-supported motion sources. TRoPE derives spectral features from the graph Laplacian and constructs pairwise topological relations from distances between multi-scale diffusion features, using these relations to modulate vertex--anchor attention through rotary phases. We further introduce a vertex-wise tail-risk objective that emphasizes persistently high-error vertices to explicitly suppress sticking artifacts. To better characterize and quantify these failures, we introduce MeshSticking-4D, a dedicated benchmark for this failure mode. Experiments on MeshSticking-4D show that TRoPE suppresses sticking artifacts while improving overall motion accuracy. Compared with the baseline, it reduces the worst-vertex error by 39.4% and lowers the separation failure rate from 49.35% to 34.90%. Additional evaluations on Motion-80 and Motion324-Subset show lower tail errors on diverse objects, while transfer to Mesh4D demonstrates applicability across backbones.

5. Profile-aware assessment of block-diagonal Hessian approximations in neural networks
Authors: Maxim Bolshim, Alexander Kugaevskikh
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01335v3
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Unclear
Relevance score: 6.5
Abstract: Evaluating block-diagonal curvature models in neural networks involves two questions: how much curvature they omit, and what a specific correction changes at a finite step. We measure the squared off-block fraction of the parameter Hessian against references for group sizes and the observed within-group curvature profile. In CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 and a skip-free counterpart, similar initial discarded fractions accompany different profiles. At saved trained ResNet-18 weights, changing from stage to layer groups moves estimated off-block mass from below to above the profile reference. An approximate assessment of probe uncertainty conditional on fixed weights and inputs resolves this crossing; some skip-free comparisons remain unresolved. Separately, in nine saved ResNet-50/Stanford Cars states from three training seeds, we compare candidates formed from off-group Hessian action, the remaining model residual and their combination. For each state, we construct two full-Hessian-informed maps on separate geometry draws. Within each draw, all three inputs share one map. At unit scale off-group input alone raises evaluated minibatch loss relative to the base preconditioner candidate in 17 of 18 state/draw comparisons, yet the combined candidate has lower loss than the remainder-only candidate in nine of those cases; preferences vary with scale and correction-norm matching. The profiles help assess block models and alternative groupings, while same-state candidate comparisons test a particular correction without equating structural mass with training gain.
