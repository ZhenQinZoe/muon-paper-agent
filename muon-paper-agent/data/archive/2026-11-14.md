1. Maximizing Capacity Utilization of LoRA Adapters for Mitigating the Learning-Forgetting Trade-off
Authors: Tomohiro Sawada, Kartik Goyal
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17191v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 4.5
Abstract: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) often underperforms full fine-tuning, especially in heterogeneous and continual-learning settings in which substantial forgetting occurs. In general, it suffers from an undesirable learning-forgetting trade-off across diverse fine-tuning settings. We identify a potential contributor: LoRA adapters, especially high-rank ones, frequently fail to realize the available capacity afforded by the configured rank. Specifically, we find that standard LoRA updates have effective rank far below their configured rank, limiting how broadly the update energy is distributed. Therefore, we propose training mechanisms that optimize the original objective while maximizing adapter effective rank. Our main method adds a simple, stable, differentiable, and SVD-free Rényi-2 effective-rank regularizer to the language-modeling loss. We also connect capacity maximization to norm-bounded updates. In mixed-domain rank sweeps on three models evaluated with multiple random seeds, a penalty weight of $λ=0.01$ lowers mean Wiki perplexity at every tested rank of at least $16$ relative to unpenalized LoRA. Mean training perplexity improves or increases by at most $0.02$ in all but one model-rank comparison. Single-task and scheduling experiments further show that the balance between learning and retention depends on the configuration. We also observe that several LoRA variants in the literature also suffer from the identified capacity issue, and our approach provides a complementary axis of improvement to LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning. These results suggest that effective rank is not only a post-hoc diagnostic of adapter quality, but a trainable objective for improving the learning-forgetting frontier. Our code is available at https://github.com/tomohiro-sawada/lora-capacity-utilization

2. Natural Gradient Learning in Predictive Coding through Lateral Circuits
Authors: Lhea Beumer, Sander de Haan, Niclas Pokel, Pau Vilimelis Aceituno, Benjamin F. Grewe
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16666v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Experiments
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.0
Abstract: Predictive coding (PC) trains neural networks through local energy-minimization dynamics without depending on a global error signal that backpropagation (BP) requires. Although PC is a promising alternative algorithm for efficient learning, the computational cost of inference dynamics currently outweighs the resulting learning benefits in practice. In this work, we take inspiration from optimization theory to extract more learning benefit from each inference phase. We introduce Natural PC, which augments standard PC with a lateral circuit, enabling exact and efficient natural gradient learning locally in space and time. Our lateral circuit learns the activity correlations that determine the Fisher information matrix and implicitly computes its inverse, avoiding expensive matrix inversion. Across all tested image reconstruction benchmarks, Natural PC achieves state of the art in terms of sample and compute efficiency compared to existing PC approaches. Most notably, Natural PC scales to ImageNet and matches the performance of BP trained with AdamW and various second-order optimizers at an order of magnitude lower computational cost while also using two orders of magnitude fewer training samples. Our results demonstrate that local natural gradient learning makes PC competitive with BP in both sample efficiency and computational cost in reconstruction tasks.

3. Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards
Authors: Jiacheng Xu, Shuo He, Fuxiang Zhang, Chaojie Wang, Bo An
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17287v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) typically relies on a verifier that can reliably judge whether sampled solutions are correct. However, in many realistic settings, complete verification is generally unavailable, while cheap checks can only falsify failures. We formalize this regime as one-sided verifiability (OSV): verified negatives are reliable counterexamples, but verified positives are merely non-falsified candidates and may contain many false positives. This asymmetry makes standard RLVR unreliable, because reinforcing verified positives can directly reward shortcuts that pass partial checks. To address this issue, we propose Negative-Only Policy Optimization (NOPO), which treats verified positives as unlabeled and updates the policy only by suppressing trusted verified negatives. Specifically, NOPO combines a reference-relative negative suppression loss with a group-level adapter that scales each update by the proportion of non-falsified responses. Across unit-test, constraint-check, and LLM self-verifiers, NOPO improves pass@k over baselines on code, logic, and math benchmarks when oracle rewards are unavailable. These gains persist under inference-time scaling and cross-benchmark transfer.

4. Ballad: Bandit-Based LLM Routing for Automated Heuristic Discovery
Authors: Samidha Verma, Ankit Anand, Sayan Ranu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17241v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 5.0
Abstract: Modern automated heuristic discovery (AHD) systems increasingly rival hand-crafted heuristics by leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to iteratively mutate and refine solutions. A natural assumption is that more powerful and more expensive models consistently yield better heuristics. We challenge this empirically: across multiple NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems, we find no consistent advantage for larger, costlier models. This raises a deeper question: given a pool of LLMs spanning frontier and freely hosted or open-weight models, how should one adaptively select which model to invoke at each step of the search? The challenge is non-trivial. The optimal LLM may shift over the course of a run, and because one LLM's output seeds the context for subsequent calls, each model's reward signal is non-stationary and entangled across the heuristic population, thus violating the independence assumptions of classical online decision-making settings. We address this through Ballad (Bandit-based LLM Routing for Automated Heuristic Discovery), an online bandit algorithm that learns to orchestrate a pool of LLMs into an ensemble that matches or exceeds what any soloist LLM achieves alone. This orchestration is powered by an LLM selection policy that favors rare, high-scoring heuristics over stable averages, a DAG-based credit propagation mechanism through the genealogy of the heuristic population, and discounting of stale observations to remain attuned to each model's current utility. Across five NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems and a pool of four models of varying capability and cost, Ballad not only matches the best individual LLM in hindsight but, in several cases, surpasses every individual model while reducing API cost by up to 65.6% relative to always using the strongest model.

5. Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks: Evidence from Additive Analogies in Meta-Learned Parameter Spaces
Authors: Abigail L. Tenenbaum, R. Thomas McCoy
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17314v1
Category: Not Relevant
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 4.5
Abstract: Humans can learn rapidly from limited experience, while neural networks typically require extensive training. In this work, we investigate what properties of a neural network can enable it to learn rapidly. We study neural networks that have been optimized via meta-learning, a technique that exposes a network to many related functions to facilitate its subsequent learning of similar functions. Prior work has shown that these meta-learned networks can learn new functions rapidly, but how they do so remains unclear. To investigate their internal structure, we take inspiration from classic work demonstrating additive relationships among the learned vectors that neural networks use to represent words (e.g., "king" minus "man" plus "woman" approximates "queen"). Analogously, we look for additive relationships among a meta-learned network's "representations" of different functions; when a meta-learned network rapidly learns to implement a specific function, all of its adapted parameter values can be concatenated into a single vector that we treat as representing the function. We find that such parameter vectors can be added and subtracted according to symbolic relationships among the functions they represent, yielding new parameter vectors that successfully represent the functions predicted by the corresponding symbolic manipulations: the networks specified by these artificially recombined vectors actually implement the intended functions without any direct training on them. Our results suggest that meta-learning can tune a network's starting point to organize subsequent learning along systematic, functionally meaningful trajectories through the space of possible parameter vectors, providing a geometric basis for realizing inductive biases in neural networks.
