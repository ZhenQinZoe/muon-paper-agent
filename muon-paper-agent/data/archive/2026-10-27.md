1. Last-Iterate Stationarity of Muon with Finitely Many Newton-Schulz Iterations
Authors: Ping Li, Vincent Y. F. Tan
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34158v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 17.0
Abstract: Muon has achieved strong empirical performance in large-scale language-model training, motivating a better theoretical understanding of its practical implementation. Unlike the idealized polar-factor update, practical Muon uses a fixed number of quintic Newton--Schulz iterations. Existing analyses mainly establish average- or best-iterate stationarity guarantees, leaving last-iterate convergence less understood. We study last-iterate gradient convergence of practical finite-step Muon on smooth, lower-bounded nonconvex objectives. For deterministic gradients, we show that without input stabilization, Muon can cycle even on a smooth convex quadratic. With positive stabilization, we establish last-iterate gradient convergence for any fixed momentum coefficient below one and a sufficiently small constant learning rate, together with square-summability of gradients. For stochastic gradients, we first show that persistent bounded, unbiased noise can prevent convergence with fixed learning rates and momentum, even with positive stabilization. We then establish almost-sure and mean-square last-iterate gradient convergence by combining decreasing learning rates with increasing momentum under bounded-variance stochastic gradients. Finally, we construct a smooth counterexample showing that neither convergence theorem guarantees a pointwise polynomial rate. We further establish density-one convergence rates, guaranteeing polynomial gradient decay for all but a vanishing fraction of iterations.

2. TUON: A Tensor-Aware Optimizer Utilizing Tensor-SVD and Polarization for Large Language Model Pretraining
Authors: Anda Tang, Xuanlin Yang, Junjie Wang, Zhouchen Lin
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35130v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory+Experiments
Paper type: Theory + Experiment
Relevance score: 13.5
Abstract: Large scale language model~(LLM) pretraining is increasingly bottlenecked by optimizer efficiency, driving optimizer design from vector-aware methods such as AdamW to matrix-aware methods. Matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon exploit matrix structure but treat layers independently, neglecting cross-layer correlations. Tensor-aware optimizers are therefore needed, yet existing ones rely on a matricization-based tensor algebra and remain restricted to shallow stacking and specific modules. In this paper, we propose \textbf{TUON}, a novel optimizer built on a transform-based tensor algebra derived from the Tensor-SVD (T-SVD). Unlike methods that polarize a globally unfolded matrix, TUON mixes layers via a unitary transform $\boldsymbol{Q}$ and then polarizes each transformed slice, and inverts the transform to recover the update. This mix-then-separate strategy captures cross-layer correlations while preserving per-layer diversity, avoiding the structural dimension imbalance and cross-layer misalignment of unfolding. Thus it successfully scales to more layers and diverse modules beyond QKV. Theoretically, we prove that TUON performs steepest descent under a $\boldsymbol{Q}$-transformed tensor spectral norm, and identify its update as the tensor polar factor of the T-SVD. We further establish transform non-redundancy, norm and smoothness comparisons with Muon and TEON, and a convergence guarantee. Empirically, TUON improves consistently over Muon and TEON on GPT-124M/350M and Qwen-0.6B/4B, with ablations confirming the transformation mechanism, cross-layer structure beyond two-layer stacking, adaptability beyond QKV, and robustness to the polarization method.

3. A Unified Analysis of Importance-Corrected Momentum Actor-Critic over a Markovian Trajectory
Authors: Yanjie Dong, Zhou Su, Wei Wang, Gang Wang
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33477v1
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 13.5
Abstract: We present a generic actor-critic method that combines a ridge-regularized pathwise least-squares temporal-difference (LSTD) critic with importance-corrected momentum (ICM) actors. The critic solves a ridge-regularized system per frame by Newton-Schulz inversion and needs no stepsize, no knowledge of the mixing time, and no rank condition. Averaging stochastic policy gradients over frames of $T$ consecutive transitions, the ICM actors incur a frame-dependent gradient bias of order $\frac{1}{T}$. To our knowledge, we establish the first finite-time bounds for ICM actor-critic methods with normalized and unnormalized steps along a single Markovian trajectory. Up to a stationary-to-discounted distribution mismatch, the averaged gradient norm over $K$ updates is bounded by ${\cal O}(K^{-1/3}+\frac{1}{T})$ for normalized steps and by ${\cal O}(K^{-1/4}+\frac{1}{T})$ for unnormalized steps. We specialize these bounds to Muon, normalized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum, SGD with momentum, and AdaNorm, and compare them in the nuclear norm. With a dimension-free stepsize, Muon confines the factor $\min\{m,n\}^{1/2}$ associated with an $m\times n$ actor parameter matrix to the noise and correction terms. The comparison also characterizes when orthogonalization guarantees a larger noiseless ascent than normalization: exactly when the gradient's nuclear rank exceeds the spectral-to-Frobenius curvature ratio.

4. Understanding Practical Optimization in Machine Learning
Authors: Egor Shulgin
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35209v1
Category: Muon-Adjacent Theory
Paper type: Theory
Relevance score: 12.0
Abstract: Modern machine learning relies on practical optimization methods that often differ from the algorithms studied in classical analyses: they train submodels, clip or normalize gradients, use layer-wise non-Euclidean directions, or approximate costly inner computations. This thesis analyzes the objective, assumptions, update, and computation defined by these procedures, rather than those of simplified proxies. For submodel training, we show that Independent Subnetwork Training can converge to a shifted fixed point because sketching before differentiation biases the update for the original objective. Model-Agnostic Sparsified Training instead incorporates the sketch distribution into its objective, which makes sketched gradients unbiased and admits convergence guarantees. For private optimization, we show that adaptive quantile clipping retains bias at any fixed quantile, while suitable quantile schedules recover near-optimal nonconvex rates under heavy-tailed noise. We then combine smoothed normalization with error compensation and obtain distributed private convergence guarantees under standard assumptions, without assuming bounded gradients. For optimizers based on linear minimization oracles, we develop and validate a layer-wise generalized smoothness model and build on it the Gluon framework for Muon- and Scion-style methods. We also show that the approximate orthogonalization in Muon matters: less precise orthogonalization calls for smaller stepsizes, so its precision should be tuned together with the learning rate. Overall, practical methods are better understood when theory retains the features that determine their optimization behavior.

5. Amplitude-Only FFN Intervention for Tool-Structured LLM Inference: Method, Gated Evaluation Protocol, and Cross-Model Empirical Results
Authors: Sheng Xu, Zhen Chen, Junhua Wang, Boyuan Huang, Ke Jia, Jiadun Zhu
Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11183v5
Category: Muon Core
Paper type: Experiment
Relevance score: 8.0
Abstract: We study amplitude-only rescaling of feed-forward network (FFN) activations to repair tool calls and other structured outputs, using multiplicative coordinate masks rather than additive steering vectors. Amplitude Gating (AG) keeps pretrained weights fixed and applies magnitude/entropy-based masks at six SwiGLU sites, with a Newton-Schulz windowed ranking variant. Across three Qwen checkpoints and six text/tool endpoints, effects are task dependent. Under frozen local scorers, Qwen3.5-9B non-NS configurations improve two function-call endpoints by +2.61/+2.67 percentage points (pp) and static ToolBench in/out-domain by +1.64/+2.82 pp; JSON gains are small. These gains survive comparing the weaker of two observed AG runs with the stronger baseline, supporting observed-run robustness rather than population significance. Scores measure lexical or answer/action-name agreement, not executed-tool success. Under the current experimental conditions, the six-task random-forest refit improves native score by +0.46 pp with a positive conditional paired interval; transfer to other settings requires further evaluation. The results support task-specific interventions; causal mechanisms, equal-budget advantages, repeated-run uncertainty, and serving cost require further evaluation. Code and experimental data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23654706.
